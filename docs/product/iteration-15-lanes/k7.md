## What was built

### F2 — Hire your partner

- **`.hirePartner`**: a row on the partner card from the `partner` stage
  on. It is built the way `FriendSystem.hire` builds a friend. Role and
  skills come from `partnerAppearanceSeed` (the `Friend.derivedSkills`
  shape, `PartnerDerivation`). The id is the partner's address-book
  contact id when they came from the address book, else one derived from
  the seed. A `Candidate` goes through `EmployeeSystem.hire` (J2's
  standing ask applies), and `founderBond` is seeded from affection.
  `FamilyState.partnerEmployeeID` (decoded if present, encoded only when
  set) is the one new save field.
- **Fair pay** is `balance.fairWeeklyPay(for:)` on a stand-in employee,
  not the friend formula. The friend formula came in under the band, and
  the roster flagged the partner "Underpaid" on day one.
- **Coupling 1, morale:** the partner's morale target moves by
  `(affection − 50) × 0.3`. This is one marked line in
  `EmployeeSystem`'s target sum, `+ state.partnerMoraleTargetDelta(...)`,
  which is exactly 0 for everyone else.
- **Coupling 2, pace:** affection moves with the office, on top of the
  schedule's drift. Crunch pace costs −0.6 a day, a launch is +6 the
  next day, and a burnout −8 on the day (`partnerOfficeAffectionDrift`,
  inside my own `RelationshipSystem`). The card prints
  **"−0.6 a day: the office"** during crunch.
- **The household draw:** their pay comes home to the wallet weekly.
  `founderPayExcess` adds it to the founder's salary against the team
  median, with one marked line. The card prints
  "Household draw $X/wk against a team median of $Y."
- **A breakup is a resignation the same day**, whichever way it happens:
  - `InteractionSystem.breakUp` (and through it the divorce and "Pack a
    bag") calls `partnerLeavesPayroll`, which follows the ordinary quit
    path: roster, friendships, and an alumni entry closed as `.lost`.
  - The relationships meter's own breakup in
    `LifeSystem.checkThresholds` is caught the same day, by my marked
    pair after `checkThresholds`.
  - There is also a daily safety net in `RelationshipSystem.run`.
- **The settlement clause:** if the partner worked at the company during
  the relationship, `FamilyDrama.equityToEx(…, ignoresMarriedDays:)`
  drops the `equityMarriedDays` threshold and counts every year. The
  divorce sheet says "They worked at the company: N% of it goes with
  them, however long the marriage."
- A heart chip on the Team roster row (a marked pair in `TeamScreen`).

### F4 — The ex on the cap table

- **The slice on the marriage card:** after a divorce that gave equity,
  the card shows the slice and its value today (`points/100 ×
  companyValuation`).
- **Buy them out:** priced at ×1.15 that value, paid wallet first and
  company cash for the rest (the `CrimeSystem.settle` split), with a
  ledger line.
  - Refusals: "Not with a case open"; "Need $X more between you and the
    company"; "It would leave under 4 weeks of runway".
  - The price and where the money comes from are printed on the button,
    with the alternative under it: "Or let it ride: their share of every
    exit, and a price that grows with the company."
  - `FamilySettlement.boughtOutDay` records it.
- **The ex as a contact:** at the settlement the ex becomes a contact
  with the new `ContactOutcome.formerPartner`, rapport = affection at the
  divorce, and archetype and skills from their seed.
  - It reuses the existing contact when there is one (the address book,
    or the alumni entry the resignation just wrote); otherwise the id is
    derived from the seed. No draw.
  - `FamilySettlement.exContactID` records it. `Contact.isOpen` counts
    `.formerPartner` as open, so talking, recruiting and asking out work
    at the existing gates.
  - The marriage card links to their contact sheet ("Nora is in your
    address book · rapport 72").
- **Old saves:** a settlement signed before this round shows the slice
  and the buy-out, with "They changed their number" in place of the
  contact (screenshot `ex-oldsave`).
- **"Pack a bag" carries the projected slice:** "…the settlement follows
  · they take 4.6% of the company ($26,591 today)". It shows on both the
  queue's decision sheet (a marked pair in `QueuePrompts` and one call in
  `DecisionSheet`) and the family room's confrontation sheet.

**Repo fact (the repo wins):** "Pack a bag" called `InteractionSystem.breakUp`, which made the
founder single, and `familyDivorceRefusal` then said "There is nobody to
divorce." The settlement its own line promised never followed. Printing
a slice on that button would have been a lie. So `.leave` now keeps a
`FamilyLeaving` snapshot on `FamilyDramaState`: married days, name,
seed, affection, contact id, and whether they worked here. The divorce
runs from the snapshot. The breakup still happens at once, as before.

### F5 — The diary reads the roadmap

- Each announce row carries a diary line when a dated diary entry falls
  within ±2 days of it: "Your anniversary with Nora is the day after".
  The engine query is `GameState.announceDiaryClash`.
- **Repo fact:** there is no `AnnounceOption` type in the repo. The
  sheet's private `Option` gained `clash`, and the query lives beside the
  other announce queries.
- **Ship day:** one marked line at the end of `ProductSystem.ship` raises
  `diary_launch_clash` when a dated entry is within ±1 day. It returns at
  once for a run with no life entries.
- The launch-day sheet gains *Keep the date* (a marked pair in
  `LaunchDaySheet`; the view is in my `Announce/` folder). It costs no
  launch party: launch hype ×0.85 on `ReleaseInfo.hypeAtLaunch`, and the
  week's launch-party vice gain is taken back for that launch (a marked
  pair in `AssetsSystem.runViceWeek`). The date counts as kept: the
  beat's own first answer without the evening, a child's birthday
  memory through `ChildhoodSystem.kidBeatResolved`, and next year's date
  goes in the diary.
- **The default is the launch:** the diary asks on the day. A child's
  birthday fires as its launch-week twin `diary_birthday_launch` (gated
  on the clash flag, appended to `LifeEvents.json`); its answer moves
  the bond and the memory the way the ordinary birthday's does.

### F7 — The doctor's letter

- **Trigger:** doors armed, health under 40, and none in the last 180
  days. A `doctor_letter` is then scheduled 7 days out, and lands with
  "Your health is at N. At this rate you will be in a hospital bed in
  about D days." (from today's drift), or "It is not falling at the
  moment…". The choices are `doctor_says`'s: *Do the three things* (an
  evening, −$400, health +18) or *After this quarter* (health −8).
  `doctor_says` is untouched. Cooldown is 180 days
  (`narrative.cooldowns`, no new field).
- **Repo facts:**
  1. `diaryContentIsWired` requires every *dated* beat to cost affection
     −20 on the miss, so the letter is an undated scheduled follow-up,
     not a diary entry.
  2. The content suite allows only `{partner}`, `{child}` and
     `{company}` tokens in catalog copy, so the engine prepends the
     numeric sentence to the JSON's body.
  3. `FamilyCalendar.isWaitingForSlot` keeps the ordinary follow-up path
     off it, so only `DoctorLetter` fires it, with its numbers.
- **Crossing, not strictly:** my one pair in `LifeSystem` sits after
  `checkThresholds`, where yesterday's health is gone. So the trigger is
  "under 40 with nothing pending and 180 days clear" rather than a
  strict downward crossing. Hospital discharge resets health above the
  line, so each stay re-arms it.
- Studio fixture note: the founder at health 17 is not falling, because
  the office's amenities offset the normal schedule's −0.2. The letter
  says so honestly (screenshot).
