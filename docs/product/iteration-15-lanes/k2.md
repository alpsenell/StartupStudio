# K2 — product lifecycle (sunset and v2; a price change is news)

*Iteration 15, lane K2, branch `k2-lifecycle` from `scaffold-15`. Spec:
`iteration-15-pm/company.md` §1 (C1) and §6 (C6). Rules 1–24.*

## What was built

### Engine (`Packages/TycoonEngine`)

Three new `GameAction` cases in the K2 region, each sent only by a player's
tap in the app; no bot sends any of them.

- **`sunsetProduct(productID)`** — a live product goes off the market by the
  player's hand: `offMarket = true`, `subscribers = 0`, `ReleaseInfo.sunsetDay`
  written, hosting stops (the weekly post skips off-market releases), and the
  topic loses its live presence through `StandingSystem.liveTopicIDs`, which
  already reads `offMarket` — so standing fades `decayWeeklyLoss` (1.5) a week
  unless something else sells there. Anyone on its support desk goes back to
  idle. Refused while a patch is in flight on it. Event `lifecycleSunset`.
- **`shipReplacing(productID, parentID)`** — ships the build exactly as `ship`
  does, but with the parent left out of `launchMarketScale`'s saturation and
  genre-fatigue counts; then retires the parent the same day and opens the
  successor's book with `lifecycle.successorBookCarry` (0.4) of the parent's
  subscribers (a one-time product carries `successorHypeCarry` 0.5 of the
  parent's `liveHype` instead). `Product.parentID` is written; the parent's
  support desk moves to the successor; the frozen launch forecast quotes the
  saturation actually applied. Same type and topic only. Events: `shipped`,
  `reviewsIn` (from `ship`, so launch day opens as always) and
  `lifecycleReplaced`.
- **`repriceProduct(productID, tier)`** — the priced price change. A rise on a
  subscription product churns `riseChurn` (12%) of the book the day it lands;
  on a one-time product the next `riseWeeks` (4) sell ×`riseUnitsFactor` (0.8).
  A cut is the quarter's sale when one is due (`saleIntervalDays` 91): one
  bumper week at `saleBump` (×1.5, the better of it and a patch's bump, never
  both) and `saleStanding` (+2) in the topic. Changes are `changeCooldownDays`
  (28) apart. Event `lifecyclePriceMoved`. A cut to budget during a category
  challenge counts as the `.budgetPrice` defence, as it always did, because the
  challenge reads the tier.

`setPriceTier` is untouched: the rivals' price-war match and restore, the
`.budgetPrice` defence and the test-target `RivalFightBots` all still call the
free, silent move. The priced change is a new case rather than a new branch of
the old one, so nothing that sends `setPriceTier` can write the new fields.

Also:

- `ReleaseInfo.sunsetDay`, `lastPriceChangeDay`, `priceRiseUntilDay`,
  `lastSaleDay` and `Product.parentID`: decode-if-present, encoded only when
  set (the synthesized encoder writes optionals with `encodeIfPresent`;
  `Product`'s hand-written encoder uses `encodeIfPresent`).
- `ProductSystem.launchMarketScale(…, excluding:)` and
  `ship(…, excludingFromSaturation:)`: defaulted `nil`, reached only from
  `shipReplacing`. `ship`'s case and bytes are unchanged. K6's factor in
  `applyDailyProgress` and K7's flag on the ship day have their room; neither
  function was restructured.
- `postWeeklySales` reads the sale bump and the rise drag only off
  `lastSaleDay` / `priceRiseUntilDay`; with both `nil` the arithmetic is the
  old one (the bump falls back to `updateBump`, the drag multiplies by exactly 1).
- `Lifecycle.swift`: the public reads the app prints and the systems gate on —
  `LifecycleRefusal` (with the player's sentence), `lifecycleReplaceableParents`,
  `lifecycleCarry`, `lifecyclePriceChange`, `lifecycleWeeklyHosting`,
  `lifecycleTopicStillHeld`, `Product.releaseInfo`.
- Balance: one defaulted property `lifecycle: LifecycleBalance = .default` in
  the K2 region, the struct in `Balance/BalanceConfig+Lifecycle.swift`, the
  `"lifecycle"` block appended at the end of `Balance.json`.
- `LifecycleDebugSeed` (DEBUG only, behind `.lifecycleDebug`): `successor`
  (a ship-ready v2 of the live product with the biggest book) and `solvent`
  (+$500k so a screenshot pass is not stopped by the studio fixture's
  bankruptcy warning).

No new `rng`/`worldRNG` draws: sunset and reprice draw nothing; a replacement
draws exactly what `ship` draws.

### App

- **Product page** (`ProductDetailScreen`): the live-ops card's segmented price
  picker became a *Change the price · Standard* button (it prints the day the
  next change is allowed while the cooldown runs) opening the priced sheet; a
  new **Lifecycle** card under it with *Build its v2* (the carry printed:
  "94 of 235 subscribers carry") and *Retire Round 6* ("Saves $162/wk · 235
  subscribers leave · Health is still held"). A retired product says when; a
  successor names what it replaced.
- **The priced sheet** (`LifecyclePriceSheet`, shared by the product page and
  the storefront's buy button): a price list on pixel paper (what a rise costs
  this product, what a cut buys, the 28 days, the challenge note when a rival
  is contesting the topic), then one row per other tier — *Raise to Premium ·
  $40 / mo* over "A rise: 28 subscribers leave the day it lands", *Cut to
  Budget* over "A sale: +50% for a week and +2 Health standing" or "Just a
  lower price: the next sale is on day 491" — and *Keep it*. The tap on the
  row is the confirmation. Rows dim with the reason during the cooldown.
- **The retire sheet** (`LifecycleRetireSheet`): a discontinuation notice on
  pixel paper — hosting back, last week's sales gone, the book leaving, the
  topic's standing, what a v2 would have carried — then *Retire it · saves
  $162/wk* or *Keep selling it*.
- **Ship confirmations** (product page and the Products list): beside *Ship it*,
  one *Replace Round 6 · 94 subscribers carry* per qualifying parent, and the
  message gains the third answer ("Running both keeps both books and both
  bills").
- **New-product flow**: `NewProductFlow(engine:successorOf:)` pre-fills type,
  topic, the type's codebase and "Round 6 v2", and opens on the details step;
  the type step gains an **Or a v2 of…** row of chips for every released
  product (newest first) with the same pre-fill.
- **Launch day**: a successor's sheet gains *Replaces Round 6 — 94 subscribers
  came across on day one.*
- **Storefront**: a retired product's buy button reads *Discontinued* with
  "Discontinued on <date>. Nobody can buy this any more."; a live sale reads
  *On sale this week only*; a recent rise says when the price went up.
- **The paper** (`NewspaperComposer`, K2 block in `leadRank`): a replacement
  ranks +15 (like an announcement), a retirement or a price move +10, so the
  week's product news leads when nothing louder happened. The lines come from
  `EventCopy`'s K2 region (two sentences where there is a cost, so the deck is
  the cost).
- `LiveOps.reprice` and `LiveOps.lifecycleConsequence` (the row's cost line).

## The "how it fails" check (measured first)

C1: *if replace-and-carry always beats keep, drop the carry to 0.25.* Measured
with a scratch harness outside the repo (not a test): the real
`shipReplacing` on the fixture, the successor's four reviews pinned, the
company given +$5M so the run lasts a year (unplayed, the studio fixture goes
bankrupt around week 10–20 and the columns freeze). "Net" is the category's
revenue minus its hosting that week; Σ is cumulative.

days old):

**Campus fixture, Round 9** (SaaS · Productivity, 2,091 subscribers, review
63, 357 days old): replacing never catches keeping, at any review and either
carry, inside 52 weeks — keep ends the year at 2,854 subscribers and Σ$3.47M;
the best successor (carry 0.4, review 80) at 602 and Σ$0.92M. The successor
launches into a crowded topic (the campus has web apps in every topic, so its
saturation sits near the floor) and its book shrinks.

**Verdict: the successor does not win inside 10 weeks whatever its review —
it does not win inside 10 weeks at any review. The carry stays at 0.4.** A
v2 beats keeping only when it is clearly better (review 70–80 against 60) and
only after four to eight months, which is the trade the feature is meant to
offer.

**A fact the spec got wrong (the repo wins):** Round 6 is not "tired". The PM
wrote the parent's book "decaying at 6% − 3% × 0.6 a week", which leaves out
acquisition: at 85 days old Round 6 still signs about 50 a week against 4.2%
churn and grows from 235 to 657 in a year if kept. On both fixtures, subscription
books grow while acquisition runs. The cases where retiring or replacing is
right are the ones the spec's "keep" column already names — a product that
costs more in hosting than it earns, a bad review, a topic at the saturation
floor. The third answer (ship the v2 *beside* the parent, no carry, both
bills) is the strongest of the three on Round 6 (Σ$122k at week 10 against
$74k keeping), because the player's own products do not split demand — the
self-cannibalisation gap the PM's cut list records. That is not K2's to fix.

**The cheap tell** (replace on every ship where a parent qualifies, against
never, 730 days, three seeds):

|---|---|---|---|
| saas-builder, never | $382,377 | $575,537 | bankrupt (both) |
| saas-builder, replace | $366,552 (37 replaced) | $550,751 (22) | bankrupt (both) |

Replace-always is not the new one answer: it costs the SaaS builder 4% of its
cash, and for the neglectful bot (one-time products, where replacing mostly
removes a saturation term) it is a coin flip across seeds. No treadmill.

## Deviations from the spec

- The priced change is a new action `repriceProduct` rather than a new
  branch of `setPriceTier`: the old action is sent by the rivals' code and by a
  test-target bot, so a branch could not tell a player's tap from theirs.
  Only the app's sheet sends the new one.
- A subscription rise churns the book the moment it lands (the button can then
  print the exact number) rather than at the next weekly post.
- A sale's bump and a patch's bump do not stack: the week takes the better.
- Retiring sends the support desk back to idle; replacing moves it to the
  successor. The spec is silent; a desk on a dead product helps nobody.
- Running both (the third answer) needs no code; it is the plain *Ship it*.

## Follow-ups

- **War room ship button** (`WarRoomScreen`, not K2's): it ships with the
  plain `.ship`; a *Replace* answer there would need its owner.
- **C6's playtest count**: once there are saves, count rises against cuts;

  real fix moves every pacing bot and needs a re-pin round.
