*Iteration 17, lane T7, branch `t7-press` from `scaffold-17`, simulator

## What was built

**The exclusive (P3).** On the ship confirmation, *Exclusive to…*: one of
the four review outlets, or none (`Screens/Products/ExclusiveRow.swift`,
mounted on the product page's and the Products list's ship card and in the
war room above its ship button). The row prints both answers: "TechDaily's
verdict leads launch day and the paper. TechDaily warms +5, the other three
cool −2: their scores follow on your next launch" against "Even-handed: no
outlet goes first and no one's standing moves." After the ship goes through
the host sends `.grantExclusive(productID:outlet:)`, so the choice composes
with every way a build ships. On launch day that outlet's card comes first
with an `EXCLUSIVE` stamp, and the paper leads with its verdict and quotes
its blurb. Every outlet's standing is clamped to ±20 and adds
`standing / 3` to its score on every later launch. Each launch moves every
standing 1 toward 0. Once any outlet has a standing, every review card
prints a byline: "warm to you · +4 on your scores", "friendly",
"even-handed", "cool on you", "cold on you".

- Engine: `Company.pressStanding: [String: Double]` (encoded as an
  outlet-sorted array, and only when non-empty) and
  `ReleaseInfo.exclusiveOutlet: String?` (not written while nil).
  `Press.swift` holds `pressScoreOffset`, the `PressStandingBand` words and
  `pressExclusiveRefusal`. `PressSystem` holds `grantExclusive` and
  `driftAfterLaunch`.
- `ship`: two adjacent marked pairs in T2's `ProductSystem.swift` — see
  Deviations.
- App: `ReviewRevealList` puts the exclusive first; `ReviewCardView` gains
  the byline and the stamp; `PressByline` lives in `LaunchDaySheet.swift`.
  The war room's reveal passes the bylines too. `NewspaperComposer` gets a
  lead-rank block. There are four `EventCopy` lines.

**A stake in a rival (G4).** On the rival's profile, under *Acquire* and
K4's paper rows, *Or buy a stake* offers three buttons, 5%, 10% and 25%,
each priced at `valuation × 1.1 × percent`. They are refused inside an
earn-out, with the for-sale sign up, after the ending, while you already
hold a stake in them, or without the cash. The caption prints what the 25%
stake does now and later: the strength it gives them, this week's
dividend, the roadmap from 10%, the poach and war effects, the 0.9×
sell-back and the fold.

Buying posts to the ledger, adds `price × 0.25 / 4000` to their strength
and records a `RivalStake { rivalID, percent, paid, sinceDay, dividends }`.

While held:
- **Dividend:** a weekly dividend is posted to the ledger ("Dividend: 25%
  of Vantage Point") on the rivals' weekly pass, after the folds.
- **Roadmap:** from 10%, "Their next launch: Fitness" appears on the
  profile (`focusTopicIDs.first`, the same line the mole's report reads).
- **Poaching:** their poaching odds are ×0.5. This applies only when they
  are the poacher, and the draw is unchanged.
- **Price war:** they start no price war with you, and their weeks-beaten
  count resets.

*Sell 25% for $X* is behind a confirmation. A fold removes the stake with a
`rivalStakeLost` event. `acquireRival` costs `(1 − percent) ×` the price;
the profile's acquisition terms mirror it, and absorbing the rival closes
the stake. The Rivals segment leads with a *Your stakes* section (a *What you
hold* card, one line per stake) once one is held, and Finances shows a *Stakes in rivals* card with value today, what
was paid, dividends and the sell-back.

- Engine: `RivalStakes.swift` (the stake, the quote, the reads) and
  `Systems/RivalSystem+Stakes.swift` (buy, sell, the week, the fold, the
  absorb). `RivalsState.stakes` is encoded only when non-empty.
- Marked pairs in T4's `RivalSystem.swift`: `run` (the dividend),
  `evolve`'s fold pass, `poachCheck`, `acquireRival`, `absorbRival` and
  `priceWarCheck`. `priceWarCheck` calls
  `rivalMarketStakeRefusesWar`, which lives in
  `RivalSystem+RivalMarket.swift`.
- App: `Screens/Business/RivalStakeRows.swift` holds `RivalStakeRows`,
  `RivalHoldingsCard` and `RivalStakeAssetCard`, with marked pairs in
  `RivalProfileScreen`, `RivalsView` and `FinancesView`.

**Actions** (T7 region): `grantExclusive`, `buyRivalStake(rivalID:percent:)`,
`sellRivalStake(rivalID:)`, `pressStakeDebugSeed(scenario:)` (debug only).
**Events**: `pressExclusive`, `rivalStakeBought`, `rivalStakeSold`,
`rivalStakeLost` (all `.info` through the severity switch's default; the
journal files them under the company).
**Balance**: `press { exclusiveGain 5, snub 2, standingDivisor 3,
driftPerLaunch 1, cap 20 }` and `stakes { percents [0.05, 0.10, 0.25],
premium 1.1, sellBack 0.9, strengthShare 0.25, roadmapFrom 0.10,
poachFactor 0.5, countOffMarket false, dividendPayout 0.2 }` in
`BalanceConfig+Press.swift`, appended at the end of `Balance.json`.

## Measurements (first, before any tuning)

**P3 — is standing visible under the noise?** `reviewNoiseSigma` is
**6.0**, which is exactly the PM's failure threshold ("6 or more"). Each
outlet's spread against its product's mean was measured over every review
set in the committed fixtures (a Python walk over every `reviews` array):

| fixture | review sets | SD of outlet − product mean | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| release-campus-day900 | 49 | 4.99 | 29.2 |
| release-studio-day400 | 14 | 5.26 | 23.0 |
| release-bankruptcy | 23 | 5.86 | 28.5 |
| l3-family-day900 | 49 | 5.08 | 29.2 |
| all (544 reviews) | 136 | **5.19** | — |

The four outlets have no persona bias: their mean deviations are −0.35
(TechDaily), −0.01 (AppVerdict), +0.05 (The Stack Review) and +0.31
(ByteSized), with SDs of 4.5 to 5.7. ±4 at the cap sits inside one
standard deviation. The check fails, so the spec's remedy applies. The
byline says the standing in words, and the offset rises to
`standing / 3` (±6.7 at the cap, a point per three standing).

**G4 — is the stake free money?** Harness (a scratch SwiftPM executable
against this branch's engine, not committed):
- the shipped balance with rivals on;
- the ten pacing seeds;
- two pacing bots;
- at day 400, 25% of the strongest rival bought, with cash topped up to
  the price (neither bot ever has it);
- marked to market at day 730.

With the spec's dividend (the percent of their full weekly revenue):

| bot | runs | mark > paid | mark + dividends > paid | sale + dividends > paid | dividend yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CrunchHire | 8 | **3/8** | 6/8 | 5/8 | 19–199% a year |
| SaaSBuilder | 7 | **2/7** | 6/7 | 6/7 | 72–279% a year |

(Runs that ended before day 400 are excluded. One stake on seed 20 002 was
lost to a fold.)

The PM's check, mark-to-market up on more than 8 of 10, does **not** fail:
the strongest rival's valuation fell on most seeds, often by half. So the
premium stays 1.1 and the sell-back 0.9. The check the PM feared the other
way round, a dividend under 2% a year on the campus, fails the other way.
A 25% stake in the campus's Vantage Point costs $178,110 and would pay
$8,742 a week, **255% a year**. Signal & Stone would pay 136%. The stake
was free money through the dividend, not the mark. A rival's valuation
(`strength × 4000 × (1 + rep/100)`) is small beside what its products sell.

**Tune:** `stakes.dividendPayout 0.2`, the share of their takings a studio
pays out. This is a new key, not in the spec. Re-measured:

| bot | mark > paid | mark + dividends > paid | sale + dividends > paid | dividend yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CrunchHire | 3/8 | 4/8 | 2/8 | 7–31% a year |
| SaaSBuilder | 2/7 | 4/7 | 4/7 | 15–56% a year |
| campus | — | — | — | Vantage Point 51%, Signal & Stone 27% a year |

Now a stake is a bet on the studio: about half pay back within the 330
days, and the rest lose on the mark. The on-market dividend is nowhere
near 2%, so `countOffMarket` stays false. (Two harness runs end
bankrupt soon after buying, on seeds 4 242 and 55 055 for CrunchHire. The
topped-up cash was spent on the stake; that is a harness artefact, not the
game.)

## Deviations, and the repo fact

1. **The verdicts already land on launch day.** The spec says the exclusive
   outlet's review is revealed on launch day "instead of a week later", and
   that "Review copies are out. The verdicts land next week" is the whole
   of launch day. In the repo, `ProductSystem.ship` writes all four reviews
   at ship, and `LaunchDaySheet` and the war room reveal all four that day.
   The "next week" card only shows for an empty review list, which never
   happens with outlets configured. The repo wins, so the exclusive's
   day-one value does not exist. What it does:
   - the outlet's card leads the reveal with an `EXCLUSIVE` stamp;
   - its verdict and blurb lead that week's paper;
   - the standing moves.

   With the spec's +5 against −2 × 3, an exclusive nets −1 standing across
   the four outlets, about −1/12 of an average point per exclusive at
   `/3`. Only the average reaches sales and reputation, so the exclusive is
   a small price paid for the paper and for steering which outlet is warm.
   I raised this with the coordinator mid-lane. **Owner call:**
   - (a) leave it as specced;
   - (b) snub 2 → 1, so an exclusive nets +2;
   - (c) hold the three non-exclusive verdicts in the app for a week. This
     would be a default-path UX change across the launch sheet, war room,
     storefront and product page.
2. **Two adjacent pairs in `ship`, not one.** The standing offset wraps the
   noise line inside the loop:
   `nextGaussian(...) + pressScoreOffset(...)`, exactly `+ 0.0` with an
   empty map, with the same one draw per outlet. The drift is one call
   right after the loop. They are ten lines apart. The loop's draws are
   unchanged in count and order.
3. **The exclusive is its own action, sent after the ship.** It is not an
   argument on `ship`, so it merges with T2's `shipAt` / `shipReplacingAt`
   without touching their signatures. The engine only grants it on launch
   day, once, to an outlet that reviewed the build. Nothing but the ship
   confirmation sends it. A crafted action could grant it after reading the
   scores; the standing it buys is the same either way.
4. **Standing / 3** instead of 0.2 per point (the measured remedy).
   `press.standingDivisor` replaces the spec's `scorePerStanding`.
5. **`stakes.dividendPayout 0.2`** is new (the measured tune above).
6. **The stake lives in `RivalStakes.swift`**, because `Stakes.swift` is
   iteration 8's ladder. The first draft overwrote that file; it was
   restored from git before the first commit.
7. **Holdings lead the Rivals segment** (after K4's sign when it leads) and
   the asset card sits after the run rate on Finances, so both are on the
   first screen.

## Follow-ups

- **Owner call on the exclusive's worth** (Deviation 1).
- **Paper acquisitions:** `acquireRivalForStock` (K4's paper deal) closes a
  held stake without discounting the paper. Only the cash acquisition
  counts the stake.
- **Valuation:** stakes are not in `companyValuation` (it would change the
  buyout and IPO numbers). The asset card says so.
- **Replacing a live product:** the war room still has no *Replace* (K2's
  follow-up). In this tree the exclusive also only rides *Ship it*, not the
  K2 replace button; on T2's sheet it rides every answer.
- **Stake events and the journal:** the stake events are company news in the
  journal. `rivalStakeLost` might deserve `.notable`, but that switch has
  no T region.
- **A war already running:** a price war the rival started before you bought in runs its course. The stake stops the next one, not a current one (seen on the campus: Vantage Point's war in Music).
- **Next topic:** the roadmap line reads `focusTopicIDs.first`, as the mole
  does. A studio that moved in on a boom may launch elsewhere
  (`rivalMarketLaunchTopic`).
