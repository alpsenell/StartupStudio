## What was built

### G1 — the publishing deal

- **Engine: `Publisher.swift` (new).**
  - `Publisher { rivalID, rivalName, advance, share, signedDay, paidBack, clawedBack }`.
  - `PublisherRefusal`, with a sentence for each case: ended, no such
    build, shipped, already published, earn-out, void, *Nobody would
    publish this*, nobody on it, too late, and a date the press would not
    print.
  - `PublisherTerms`, the whole term sheet as arithmetic.
  - The queries `publisherCandidate`, `publisherTerms`,
    `publisherEstimate`, `publisherLaunchHype`, `publisherBuyoutPrice`,
    `publisherBuyoutBlocker`, `publisherIsActive` and `productsPublished(by:)`.
- **The counterparty** is the strongest rival with strength ≥ 30, picked
  by sort. Ties break on the id, as `dealStrongest` does.
- **The advance** is `(max(crew weekly pay, salaryBase × 3) + weeklyOperatingCost + rent) × days to the ETA / 7 × 0.5`,
  paid in cash on signing. The ×0.5 and the crew floor are the spec's two
  remedies (see Measurements).
- **The share.** 40% of every sales week, posted as its own ledger line
  (*Waypoint Nine's share: Quiet 1 −$224*). It is one T4 pair after the
  sales post in `ProductSystem.postWeeklySales`, T2's file.
- **The date** is the ETA plus 14 days, announced through
  `AnnounceSystem.announce`, the same function `.announceShipDate` reaches.
  - A slip therefore costs what J5's slip costs.
  - A slip also claws back 25% of the advance. That is one T4 pair in
    `AnnounceSystem.slip`, T5's file; it returns the slip event followed
    by `.publisherClawedBack`.
  - A build that already has a standing date keeps it, and the publisher
    signs up to it.
- **Launch day.** The publisher's name adds `launchEventHype × 0.5` (+20)
  to the hype at launch. It is one T4 pair in `ProductSystem.ship`, an
  added `+ publisherLaunchHype(…)` term that is exactly 0 without a
  publisher.
- **They read the board.** The rival adds the topic to `focusTopicIDs` the
  day the deal is signed. The copycat's four-week window comes with it:
  the build is now announced, so J5's `announceRipeDay` applies unchanged.
- **Buy them out** at `1.5 × advance − the share paid so far`, never below
  $0. It is refused without the cash, with the reason.
- **Refused** on a product that already has a publisher, on a build past
  `tooLate`, and inside an earn-out, as the spec lists. Also refused on a
  void date, and after the ending.
- **Actions:** `.shopToPublisher(productID:)` and `.buyOutPublisher(productID:)`,
  in `GameAction`'s and `Reducer`'s T4 regions. No bot sends either.
- **Events:** `publisherSigned`, `publisherClawedBack` and
  `publisherBoughtOut`, in `GameEvent`'s T4 region.
- **`Systems/PublisherSystem.swift` (new).** `shop`, `buyOut`,
  `postShare` and `clawBack`. It draws nothing.
- **`Product.publisher`.** Decoded if present, encoded only when set. The
  property and the decode and encode lines are T4 pairs in `Product.swift`
  (T2's file). The field has a default rather than a new init parameter,
  so K2's init line is untouched.
- **Balance:** `publisher { minStrength 30, share 0.4, clawbackPerSlip 0.25, buyoutMultiple 1.5, launchHypeFactor 0.5, crewFloorSalaryBases 3, advanceWeeksFactor 0.5, dateSlackDays 14, estimateWeeks 42 }`.
  - The struct is in `Balance/BalanceConfig+Publisher.swift`, with every
    key optional.
  - The property is in `BalanceConfig`'s T4 region.
  - The block is appended at the end of `Balance.json`.
- **App: `Screens/Products/PublisherSheet.swift` (new).**
  - **`PublisherRow`**, the *Financing* row on the build's Ship date card
    (`AnnounceCard`, one T4 pair in `Announce/AnnounceViews.swift`). It
    shows *Shop it to Waypoint Nine: $1,298 now* with the share and the
    date under it; or *Published by … · 40%* with the buy-out price; or
    the refusal sentence.
  - **`PublisherSheet`**, the one home for the deal.
    - Before signing it is the term sheet on pixel paper: the advance with
      its arithmetic, and the share with an estimate ("If it reviews 62,
      that is about $10,350 of its first 42 weeks").
    - It also prints the date with what a miss costs and claws back, the
      launch hype, the topic move and the copycat's four weeks, and the
      buy-out formula.
    - Two answers: *Take $1,298 from Waypoint Nine* (cash today; 40% of
      the build and the date are theirs) and *Keep it yours*.
    - After signing it shows the advance, the share paid, what clawbacks
      have cost and the date. Its two answers are *Buy Waypoint Nine out
      for $1,947* (the share stops; the date stays in print) and *Keep
      the deal*.
  - **`PublisherProfileRows`** on the rival's profile (one T4 pair in
    `RivalProfileScreen`): *They publish for you*, one row per build, each
    opening the sheet. For the studio a deal would go to, the rows point
    to where to ask.
  - **The paper** leads with a deal (+20), a clawback (+15) and a buy-out
    (+10). That is a T4 pair in `NewspaperComposer`.
  - **Journal lines** for the three events, in `EventCopy`'s T4 region.

### O1 — the sign caps the market

- **`RivalSystem.buyoutCheck`**, my file, has a T4 pair after both draws:
  `amount = strong ? dealCappedApproach(offered) : offered`. Nothing moves
  in the world stream.
  - A strategic approach capped under today's valuation is recorded as a
    sale (`lastBuyoutWasStrategic = false`), as K4's sign bids under the
    valuation already are.
  - With no sign up (every bot and every fixture) `amount == offered` and
    the flag is `strong`, exactly as before.
- **`Deals.swift`.**
  - `dealCappedApproach` is the cap itself.
  - `dealStrategicSuitor` is the studio `buyoutCheck` would actually send:
    the strongest, and only when the company qualifies.
- **The sign card (`DealViews`)** prints the other side before the tap.
  - *"While the sign stands nobody pays more than your ask. Without it,
    Vantage Point comes on its own at 1.5–2.5× — at their number, on their
    clock."*
  - The confirmation dialog adds the cap line.
  - The listed card's take-down footnote prints the reverse: *"Down, the
    market sets the price again: Vantage Point comes on its own at
    1.5–2.5×."*
- **`deals.askMax` 1.6 → 2.0**, in `Balance.json` and the default. This
  is the spec's remedy; see Measurements.

### G1 — the advance against the expected give-up

The give-up is the spec's: the type's launch-week units × `unitPrice`
through the 42-week ramp, at review 60. I took no hype and the type's own
market (no saturation), and used the fixture's marketing average for the
ramp.

**First reading, on the spec's terms** (share 0.5, whole weeks to the ETA,
no floor). Every build in the fixtures is within 0–10 days of its ETA,
except the garage's.

| fixture | build | ETA | advance | ratio to the 50% give-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| garage | Quiet 1 (solo, founder pay $0) | 23 d | $1,000 | **8%** |
| studio | Rounds 15–17 (3–4 crew, $4–6k/wk) | 0–4 d | $6–8k | 22–30% |
| campus | Rounds 50–54 (6–7 crew, $12–15k/wk) | 1–10 d | $20–39k | 78–153% |

Those builds are already past what the announce rules allow for a date
(`tooLate`, under 14 days out). So I re-measured the same crews on the
same builds from zero, which is the point where a deal is actually shopped:

| fixture | ETA from zero | ratio, spec terms | with the floor (×3 salaryBase) |
|---|---|---|---|
| garage | 61 d | **18%** | 56% |
| studio | 13–19 d | 57–89% | same |
| campus | 9–14 d | **153–180%** | same |

**Both checks fail**: the garage is under 30% and the campus is over
100%. So both of the spec's remedies ship: the crew line is floored at
`salaryBase × 3` ($540 a week), the share is 40%, and the advance counts
half the weeks to the ETA. Weeks are counted as `days / 7`, not rounded up
to whole weeks: at 13–19 days a whole week of rounding moved the studio
by 30 points.

**The shipped balance, through the engine's own `publisherTerms`**
(against a 40% give-up at review 60):

| fixture | build (from zero) | counterparty | advance | ratio | open? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| garage | Quiet 1, 61 d | Waypoint Nine (49) | $3,442 | **34%** | yes |
| garage | Quiet 1 as it stands, 23 d | Waypoint Nine | $1,298 | 13% | yes |
| studio | Round 15, 19 d | Sable & Co (45) | $10,936 | **50%** | yes |
| studio | Round 16, 19 d | Sable & Co | $8,178 | **38%** | yes |
| campus | Round 50, 14 d | Vantage Point (97) | $20,657 | 100% | yes |
| studio / campus | every other build | — | — | — | `tooLate` |

The garage and the studio land inside the 30–60% target. The campus's
only shoppable fresh build is at 100%. That is because its crews finish a
build in two weeks and the whole campus rent ($7,560) is charged to each
build; see follow-up 4.

- **Against the builds' real markets**, the saturation the forecast already
  reads (0.03–0.13 on the studio and campus, which ship web apps every
  week) makes the real give-up far smaller than the advance there. The
  deal is worth most where the company ships the least, which is the
  spec's own claim.

**End to end on the garage** (`shopToPublisher` on Quiet 1, then play it
out):

- **Signing:** the advance of $1,298 lands in the cash. The date (day 77)
  is announced, Waypoint Nine's focus becomes `[health, productivity]`,
  and a second shop is refused as `published`.
- **A forced slip** (debug build) returns `announceSlipped` then
  `publisherClawedBack`: cash −$325 (25%), and `clawedBack` 325.
- **Shipping on day 63:** hype at launch goes 0 → 20.0, which is
  `launchEventHype × 0.5`.
- **Twelve weeks later:** revenue $19,253, share paid $7,700 (40.0%) in 12
  ledger lines. The buy-out price is now **$0**; see follow-up 1.

### O1 — the wait for an unsolicited ≥1.5× offer

Measured on K4's harness shape: 52 weeks, no sign, every offer declined.

| fixture | offers ≥1.5× | first | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| studio | 2.48× | week 1 | — (a distress 1.05× at week 16, and the headless company went broke on day 525) |
| campus | 10 of 10 | week 3 | mean 4.9 weeks (2.12×, 2.39×, 1.55×, 1.58×, 2.03×, 1.65×, 1.79×, 2.37×, 1.54×, 2.24×) |

From the balance: a check every 7 days at p 0.4 puts the first approach
2.5 weeks away, and the 28-day cooldown makes the mean gap 6.5 weeks.
**Under eight weeks on both**, so the remedy applies: `askMax` 2.0.

**With the cap:** the campus with the sign at 1.2× ($2,581,264), for 30
weeks. Every unsolicited approach (weeks 3, 7, 15, 20, 26) arrived at
exactly the ask, never above it. The sign's own bids climbed 1.0× → 1.39×
and reached the ask at week 20.

## Deviations

1. **Share 0.4, the advance at half the weeks, and the crew line floored at
   $540 a week**: the spec's remedies. Both checks failed, on different
   fixtures.
2. **Weeks are `days / 7`, not whole weeks** (see Measurements).
3. **`Publisher` carries `rivalName` and `clawedBack`** beyond the spec's
   fields. The deal still names the studio after it leaves, and the sheet
   prints what slips have cost.
4. **The deal lapses with the studio.** If the publisher is acquired or
   folds, the share stops; the sheet says so. The spec does not say.
5. **A date already standing is kept**; the publisher signs up to it. A
   void date is refused, as is a date the press would not print
   (`noPrint`, unreachable today behind `tooLate`).
6. **The clawback is 25% of the original advance per slip.** The second
   slip (J5's void) claws back again.
7. **The Financing row is on the Ship date card** (J5's `AnnounceCard`, the
   card on the build's page), not inside the announce sheet. The deal's
   own sheet is its one home.
8. **O1's copy says "comes on its own at 1.5–2.5×"**, not the spec's "has
   paid": a suitor may never have come. `DealCopy.wait` now names only the
   studio `buyoutCheck` would send (the strongest). K4's copy listed every
   rival the company is worth 2× of, which was one too many on the campus.
9. **A strategic approach capped below today's valuation ends as *Sold
   up*.** This is only possible with an ask under 1.0×, and it matches
   K4's sign bids.
10. **Ledger.** The share posts as a negative `.sales` line, so net sales
    read net. The advance, the clawback and the buy-out are `.other`.

## Follow-ups

1. **The buy-out reaches $0.** The spec's `1.5 × advance − share paid`
   hits $0 as soon as the share has repaid 1.5× the advance: on the garage
   after about three sales weeks. After that "half forever" is escapable
   for nothing, which is a one-answer default. This is an owner call:
   - floor the buy-out (for example at 50% of the advance);
   - or price it off the remaining weeks' expected share;
   - or keep it and call the deal a loan repaid through the share.
2. **A released product's own page says nothing about its publisher.**
   `ProductDetailScreen` is T2's file, and `AnnounceCard` only shows in
   development. The deal stays visible in the ledger, the sheet (from the
   studio's profile) and the journal. One T2 pair putting `PublisherRow`
   on the released section would give it a line.
3. **Scroll targets.** Both new rows are below the fold on their screens.
   An `.id` for the Ship date card (T2's file) would let the route land on
   the row itself.
4. **The campus advance** charges the whole office rent to each build,
   which is why it sits at 100%. Dividing the rent by the builds in
   development would bring it to about 60%. It is not in the spec.
5. **The estimate's review** is the crew's ceiling against this year's
   expectation, which assumes a finished build. The garage estimated 62
   and shipped at the gate to 41; the copy says "if it reviews 62", so it
   is honest but optimistic.
6. **`make strings`** at merge: new strings in `PublisherSheet`,
   `DealViews` and `EventCopy`.
