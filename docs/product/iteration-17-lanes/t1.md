## The first measurement (the PM's "how it fails" check)

The spec's central claim was a grep, so I read `acceptBuyout`'s tail and the
ledger's net worth first. It holds. `acceptBuyout` does `company.cash += price`
and ends the run. `LegacyRun.record` reads `founderNetWorth` = wallet + assets
+ `equityRemaining`% of the valuation + the director's loan. Granted points
sit outside `equityRemaining` from the day of the grant, so the ledger never
counted the holders, and nothing paid them. The fix therefore goes in the exit
paths, not the ledger.

Pricing the acceleration on `release-studio-day400` (60% equity, valuation
**$572,043**, computed from the fixture with the shipped balance) at a 1.0× bid:

| Unvested points out | Acceleration costs | Founder's share | Share of the cheque |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 (the spec's case) | $17,161 | $343,226 | **5.0%** |
| 2 | $11,441 | $343,226 | **3.3%** |
| 1.5 (3 granted, half vested) | $8,581 | $343,226 | **2.5%** |
| 4 | $22,882 | $343,226 | 6.7% |

The ratio is simply unvested ÷ equity, so it is the same at any price.
Exactly 3 unvested points lands on the line. Anything already vesting, or any
2-point grant, is under it. K3's bots kept 2–6 points out, and the unvested
share only shrinks with time. **The check fails in the typical case, so the
remedy is applied:** during an earn-out, each lapsed holder reads as
`exits.lapsedEarnOutMisses` (1) missed reviews. It is capped one short of
`earnOutMissesToOust` (2), so a lapse alone never ousts you, but one real miss
afterwards does.

## What was built

**The exit reads the options and repays the director** (`Exits.swift`,
`ExitSystem.settle`). This runs at the acquisition (`acceptBuyout`'s tail), the
sell-up (`dealSellUp`), the earn-out's signing (`acceptBuyoutEarnOut`) and the
IPO (`fileIPO`), in this order:
1. The director's loan comes out of company cash into the wallet, as a ledger
   line. The valuation is unmoved, because it carried the loan as a liability.
2. Every vested option point is paid its share of the price. This is a
   0-amount ledger line, like the IPO's own. The founder's slice never held
   those points, so nothing is taken off it.
3. The unvested are the founder's answer:
   - **Accelerate:** they vest today (`grantDay` moved back) and are paid.
   - **Let them lapse:** the points return to `equityRemaining`, and the
     holder's grant shrinks to what had vested.

At an IPO the unvested always lapse. During an earn-out, a lapsed holder
leaves at the next weekly pass through `ExitSystem.run`, a new daily system:
loyalty goes to 0 and they take the ordinary `.quit` path with an alumni
entry. They also read as a missed review, per the remedy above. At every exit,
each lapsed holder adds `exits.lapsedNamePenalty` (2) to `FounderStanding.name`
for good. What happened is kept in `GameState.joins.exit` as an `ExitRecord`.

The sheets print a query, `ladderExitSplit(price:)` in `Ladder.swift`, and
`settle` pays exactly what it prints. `Employee.vestedEquity(day:balance:)` now
delegates to a new overload that takes only the grant block, with the same
arithmetic.

**Actions.** `acceptBuyout`, `acceptBuyoutEarnOut` and `sellUp` keep their old
shape and bytes: they are the lapse. There are three new cases carrying the
answer: `exitAcceptBuyout(accelerate:)`, `exitAcceptBuyoutEarnOut(accelerate:)`
and `exitSellUp(accelerate:)`. The engine functions gained defaulted
`accelerate:` arguments, and `acceptBuyout` also a defaulted `balance:`.

**The row.** The buyout sheet and the sell-up sheet both carry an OPTIONS row
on pixel paper, above the answers (`ExitOptionsRow`). It shows:
- the loan coming back first;
- the vested holders and their share;
- two answers, each printing the founder's share of the price and what it
  closes:
  - *Let them lapse*: "$337,505 to you · Priya and Aylin won't come · name +4
    · earn-out: a missed review";
  - *Accelerate*: "$326,065 to you · $11,441 vests for them · the team stays
    whole".

The sell and earn-out buttons send the exit action with the answer. With no
loan and no holder there is no row, so the prompt is identical to before.

**Firing the partner is a fight.** A guard in `EmployeeSystem.fire` (one T1
pair, after `departed`) catches the case where the person fired is
`partnerEmployeeID`:
- that day: affection −25, the payroll link cleared, the alumni entry closed,
  and a phone line;
- the next morning, via `RelationshipSystem.run`: a firing with cause (read
  from `firedWithCauseIDs`) lands the rest, −40 in all, and opens a question.

The question is a Life card, "The bag by the door", with two answers:
- **Pack a bag**: K7's snapshot plus `breakUp`, so the settlement follows;
- **Stay and take it**: affection −10, and `partnerHireBlocker` refuses
  rehiring them.

Left unanswered for 3 days, staying is the answer.

**The dividend reaches the holders' desks.** `declareDividend` now pays every
holder on payroll whose grant produced a line: morale +6 and loyalty +5 on the
day, plus a feed line. `founderMoneyHolderDividendCause(for:)` is ready for the

**The partner's morale no longer reads the dividend as pay.**

partner's own household.

**Balance.** `BalanceConfig+Exits.swift`, `"exits"` appended at the end of
`Balance.json`, every key optional.

## Deviations

- **`acceptBuyout(accelerate:)`.** The enum case cannot take a defaulted
  associated value without breaking every `.acceptBuyout` call site, the
  pinned tests included. So the case is unchanged and the answer rides on the
  three new `exit…` cases. The defaulted argument is on the engine function.
- **The old `.acceptBuyout`'s grant block.** The reducer's old call passes no
  balance (`Reducer.swift:431`, outside my regions). So `acceptBuyout` falls
  back to `GrantBalance.default`, which equals the shipped `ladder.grants`.
  **Glue:** pass `balance: balance` at `Reducer.swift:431` at merge.
- **The sell-up.** The loan and the holders are settled in
  `RivalSystem.dealSellUp`, where the money lands (one T1 pair wrapping the
  tail, plus a pair on the signature). `Deals.swift`'s price query is
  untouched, and the sheet shows the loan instead.
- **The morning question lives on the Life tab**, not in the queue. The queue
  is `Queue.swift`, T5's file.
- **J8 counts all grants**, not only options: any holder with a line (angel,
  joined-for-equity, options) who is on payroll. The co-founder's
  "rest" line is not read.
