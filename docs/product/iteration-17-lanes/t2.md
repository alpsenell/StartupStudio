## What was built

### Engine (`Packages/TycoonEngine`)

Seven new `GameAction` cases in the T2 region. Six are sent only by a
player's tap; the seventh is the DEBUG seed. No bot sends any of them, and
no old case changed shape.

- **`shelveBuild(productID)`** (O3). The build leaves `products` for a new
  `GameState.shelf`, so its slot frees today.
  - An announced date slips first, through J5's own slip: the reputation,
    the hype share and the new date, with a second slip voiding it. This
    is one call to `AnnounceSystem.forceSlip`, T5's entry point; I did not
    edit T5's file.
  - Then the hype goes to 0, everyone on the build loses 3 morale and goes
    idle, and `DevProgress.shelvedDay` is written.
  - The points keep. A new daily system, `ShelfSystem.run`, takes a
    thirteenth of 10% a week. It returns on its first line while the shelf
    is empty.
  - Event: `buildShelved` (named, because the product has left `products`).
- **`unshelveBuild(productID)`** (O3, *Take it off the shelf*). Back into a
  free slot, refused with "Every build slot is busy". Everyone idle joins
  it, the way everyone idle joins a new build. Event `buildUnshelved`.
- **`scrapBuild(productID)`** (P2, *Scrap it*). Works on a build in a slot
  or on the shelf.
  - The product is removed. The type's codebase keeps what the scrap banks
    (see "Where the specs differ") plus the build's `debtAccrued`. Everyone
    on it loses 4 morale and goes idle. There is no review and no sales.
  - Refused within 7 days of the ETA ("It is 4 days from done. Ship it or
    don't.") and while an unslipped announced date stands ("Shelve it
    first, which is the slip, or ship it.").
  - A shelved build can always be scrapped: it already paid the slip, the
    hype and the morale on the way in.
  - Event `productScrapped`. This is P2's `productShelved`, renamed because
    the drawer took the word.
- **`startProductAsV2(typeID, topicID, name, focus, codebaseID, parentID)`**
  (J4). `ProductSystem.startProduct` gains a defaulted `parentID: UUID? =
  nil`. The new case is the only caller that passes one, and the parent
  must be released and of the same type and topic.
  - The old `startProduct` and `startProductOnCodebase` cases are untouched
    (a new branch gated on a new argument), so `HouseField`'s pattern match
    and every bot's start are the bytes they were.
- **The old version** (J4). `postWeeklySales` reads
  `state.buildParentDecay(productID:)` once per product.
  - Subscription acquisition ×0.5 and churn ×1.5; a one-time product's
    units ×0.5. This applies while a product whose `parentID` is this one
    is on the market.
  - Otherwise it is exactly `(1, 1)`, and a ×1.0 multiply is bit-exact.
    `shipReplacing` is the only other writer of `parentID`, and it retires
    the parent the same day.
  - The loop, the review roll and the function's shape are unchanged. My
    additions are three marked pairs.
- **`shipAt(productID, tier)` / `shipReplacingAt(productID, parentID, tier)`**
  (P1). `ship` and `shipReplacing` gain a defaulted `tier: PriceTier =
  .standard`.
  - A launch off standard writes the tier and `lastPriceChangeDay` (K2's
    28-day clock) in one marked block after the stage flips.
  - `.ship` is untouched, and standard writes nothing.
  - `ship`'s review loop is not touched, so T4, T5, T6 and T7's pairs have
    their room.
- **`buildDebugSeed(scenario)`**, DEBUG only (`ready`, `shelve`, `v2`,
  `v2shipped`).
- **Reads** (`Build.swift`): `BuildRefusal` (with the player's sentence),
  `buildShelveQuote`, `buildScrapQuote`, `buildUnshelveRefusal`,
  `buildScrapRefusal`, `buildParentDecay`, `buildDeclaredParent`,
  `buildRunBothQuote`, `DevProgress.completion(of:)`.
- **S2's refusal** now reads "Ship or shelve N builds first", in one marked
  pair in `OfficeDowngrade.swift` (not in my ownership row; the spec names
  it).
- **Balance.** One defaulted `build: BuildBalance` in the T2 region,
  `BalanceConfig+Build.swift`, and a `"build"` block appended at the end of
  `Balance.json`:
  - `parentAcquisition 0.5`, `parentChurn 1.5`
  - `shelveCrewMorale −3`, `shelveDecayPerQuarter 0.1`
  - `scrapBankFraction 0.5`, `scrapCrewMorale −4`, `scrapLastDays 7`

**Why a shelf rather than a flag and filters.** O3 asks that
`buildsInFlight` and "the six in-flight reads" exclude shelved builds. The
tree has 44 `case .development` sites plus the bots. A shelved build that
stays in `products` would need a filter at every one of them: the daily
output, hype decay, the Fame drip, campaigns, the announce pass, espionage,
crime, the war room, the Now card, the tutorial and so on.

Moving it to `GameState.shelf` makes every one of those reads right by
construction:
- `buildsInFlight`, `hasFreeDevSlot`, `productInDevelopment` (the Now card)
  and S2's refusal count only builds in a slot. I changed none of them.
- A campaign on a shelved build finds no product and drops, which is the
  hype going.
- The sweep that idles stale assignments would have idled the crew
  anyway. The shelve does it on the tap, so T3's `EmployeeSystem` needed no
  call.

`DevProgress.shelvedDay` still exists, as the spec asked: it is the decay's
clock and the drawer's "shelved 3 weeks ago".

### App

- **The ship sheet** (`Screens/Products/ShipSheet.swift`, new; P1 + J4). It
  replaces the confirmation dialog on the product page and on the Products
  list; every answer the dialog had is a row here.
  - On pixel paper: the forecast's score and the page's pool readout.
  - Three price rows, reusing `LifecyclePriceSheet`'s row with a tick.
    Each carries `LiveOps.priceCaption` at the forecast score, plus a new
    `LiveOps.launchTierLine`: "Free today · the next change waits 28 days,
    to day 428 · ×0.85 weight in the topic's share".
  - Then *Ship it at Premium*, printed with the run-both line when there is
    a live declared parent. On the studio fixture it reads "Running both:
    Round 6 keeps its 235 subscribers and its bill, and loses about 16 a
    week to its v2." (the PM's sketch said 26; 16 is the engine's number
    at this week's acquisition and churn).
  - Then one *Replace X at Premium* row per qualifying parent.
- **The build page** (`BuildWayOutCard`, under the Ship button). *Shelve
  it* and *Scrap it* on one card, each with everything printed:
  - Shelve: "Frees a slot today · hype 24 → 0 · the announced date slips
    (−3 reputation) · 4 idle, morale −3 · 71% keeps, −7% a quarter".
  - Scrap: "Banks N points into the Round 1 codebase · 4 idle, morale −4 ·
    no review, no sales, gone for good", or its refusal, dimmed.
  - A confirmation repeats the line. The page pops once the build has
    left.
- **The drawer** (`ShelfCard` on the Products list, under the builds). Each
  shelved build shows its type, percent done, when it was shelved and what
  it loses a week, with *Take it off the shelf* (or why not) and *Scrap it*.
  A product page opened on a shelved id says "On the shelf" rather than
  "Product not found".
- **The v2 chip** (`NewProductFlow`). A v2 picked from *Or a v2 of…* or
  *Build its v2* sends `startProductAsV2`, as long as the type and topic are
  still its parent's.
- **Lifecycle card** (K2's, on the parent's page).
  - It prints "The old version: Round 6 Next sells beside it, so it signs
    50% of the subscribers it would and churns ×1.5."
  - A v2 shipped beside its parent says "shipped beside it", not "which it
    retired".
  - "Replaced by" appears only when the successor retired it on its launch
    day (`LifecycleShip.replaced`).
- **Launch day.** K2's row, which `LaunchDaySheet` shows for any product
  with a `parentID`, now draws `BesideParentLaunchRow` for a v2 shipped
  beside its parent. The change is inside my view, so T7's file was not
  touched.
- **`EventCopy`**: the three events in the T2 region.
- **Debug:** `DebugLaunch.buildScenario` and `BuildDebug` / `.buildAutoRoute`
  (one marked line in `ProductsScreen`).

## Where the two shelve specs differ, and what I chose

| | O3 (systems) | P2 (player) | built |
|---|---|---|---|
| verb | Shelve (drawer, keep progress) | "Shelve" = cancel and bank | both, as two answers on one card: **Shelve it** (O3) and **Scrap it** (P2) |
| morale | crew −3 | −4 | shelve −3, scrap −4 (each spec's own number for its own verb) |
| announced date | shelving slips it | refused while unslipped | shelve slips it; scrap refused while unslipped, so the path is shelve (the slip) then scrap from the drawer |
| last days | — | refused inside 7 days of the ETA | scrap only; shelve has no such gate, because shelving loses nothing permanent |
| bank | — | `min(pool, pts × 0.5)`; remedy caps it "at the codebase's existing points (a lineage you already have gets nothing)" | each pool rises to `min(pool, pts × 0.5)` and never stacks on the pool it already has, so the codebase keeps the better of the two |
| event | — | `productShelved` | `productScrapped` (the drawer took the word), plus `buildShelved` / `buildUnshelved` |
| J4 balance keys | `lifecycle { parentAcquisition, parentChurn }` | — | in the new `build` block; K2's struct is left alone |

On the bank: read literally, "capped at the codebase's existing points" (add
at most what the lineage already holds) gives a first-of-type scrap exactly
nothing, which is P2's headline case. It also lets a scrap loop double a
lineage, whereas `CodebaseSystem.recordShip` *sets* the pools to the last
build's 35%, so they never ratchet. Keeping the better of the two matches
recordShip and matches the remedy's parenthetical: a lineage you already
have gets nothing.

## The "how it fails" checks (measured first)

A throwaway executable in the session scratchpad (`t2measure`), not a test,
running the real engine on the bundled fixtures.

**P1: is premium a lookup?** Over the 63 fixture launches (14 on the studio,
49 on the campus, 0 on the garage; all 63 at standard):

| | |
|---|---|
| scores | min 45, median 69, max 88; 40s 6 · 50s 7 · 60s 19 · 70s 26 · 80s 5 |
| premium revenue per buyer ≥ standard | 11 of 63; from 60 up, 11 of 50 |
| premium first catches standard at | review **77** (0.48 at 60 and below, 0.64 at 65, 0.80 at 70, 0.96 at 75, 1.12 at 80, capped at 1.28 from 85) |

Premium does not win from 60 up; it wins on 11 launches, all at 77 or
above. The check passes and the decision is live. Where premium does win
on revenue, the cost the sheet prints is the standing share (×0.85
against ×1.0, and budget ×1.35) and the 28-day clock. Every row carries
it through `launchTierLine`.

**J4: does the decay make replacing the one answer?** The studio's Round 6
(SaaS · Health, 235 subscribers, review 60), a declared v2 "Round 6 Next"
started on the codebase, filled, shipped, and its four reviews pinned. The
company got +$5M so the year runs. Each row is the parent's subscribers +
the v2's, then Σ of the pair's revenue minus both hosting bills.

| path | w10 | w20 | w52 |
|---|---|---|---|
| keep (no v2) | 435 · Σ$79k | 771 · Σ$232k | 1,120 · Σ$1,069k |
| replace, carry 0.4, r60 | 0+203 · Σ$32k | 0+297 · Σ$96k | 0+595 · Σ$454k |
| **both ×0.5/×1.5, r60** | 256+95 · Σ$68k | 255+163 · Σ$164k | 460+457 · Σ$652k |
| both ×0.7/×1.25, r60 | 316+95 · Σ$76k | 349+163 · Σ$192k | 706+457 · Σ$807k |
| both ×1 (before T2), r60 | 400+95 · Σ$88k | 489+163 · Σ$233k | 1,114+457 · Σ$1,055k |
| replace r70 / both r70 | Σ$34k / Σ$69k | Σ$103k / Σ$169k | Σ$497k / Σ$690k |
| replace r80 / both r80 | Σ$35k / Σ$70k | Σ$109k / Σ$173k | Σ$544k / Σ$728k |

Replace wins at no review score and no horizon, so the spec's remedy
(soften to ×0.7/×1.25 "if replace wins at every review score") does not
apply. **×0.5/×1.5 ship.**

What the decay does: running both used to be free. Before T2 it was keep
plus the v2's own sales, Σ$1,055k at a year. Now it costs the parent 36% of
its year (Σ$652k), and the v2 beside it no longer beats simply not building
a v2 (keep Σ$1,069k). The trade the spec wanted exists: the v2 has to earn
its place.

What the decay does not do: make replacing competitive. The 40% carry
(K2's number) still loses to the parent's own 235 subscribers kept at half
acquisition. That is K2's carry, not T2's decay, and it is a follow-up.

**P2: does a shelve-at-the-gate loop beat shipping?** Every in-flight
build on the studio and the campus was pushed to the ship gate. At the gate
I compared the scrap's bank with what shipping leaves the codebase
(`recordShip` sets the pools to 35% of the shipped points), plus the
shipped build's first quarter.

| build | at the gate | lineage | ship sets the lineage to | scrap @0.5 banks | ship's review, first quarter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| studio Round 15 (web app) | 199 / 325 pts, 9.8 pts/day | 110 | 69 (−41 pts, −4.2 days) | +1 pt | 37, $4k |
| studio Round 16 | 156 / 325 | 110 | 54 (−56) | 0 | 19, $3k |
| studio Round 17 | 142 / 325 | 110 | 49 (−61) | 0 | 23, $3k |
| campus Rounds 50–54 | 100–173 / 325 | 112 | 35–60 (−51 to −77) | 0 | 5–31, $223–$1k |

The loop does not beat shipping: on every fixture build the lineage
already holds more than half of a gate build's points in every pool, so the
scrap banks nothing. **The fraction stays 0.5.** The bank is worth
something only for a type with a weak or no lineage (a first build, a new
type), where it founds one at half the build's points against the 35% a
ship would leave.

The repo disagrees with the PM on a fact: Round 15 "would bank 100 into
the web-app codebase". The codebase already holds 110, so it banks 1. What
the table does show is a cost of shipping a rough build that nobody
printed: a gate ship *lowers* the lineage (110 → 69 on Round 15, about 4
build days for the next web app). It is a follow-up, not T2's to change.

**O3: is the drawer the new one answer?** In-flight builds whose forecast
quality is under their topic's rival average: garage 0 of 1, studio 0 of 3,
campus 2 of 5 (Rounds 52 and 53, forecast 19 and 13 against rivals at
81–82). That is not most, so the decay stays at 0.1 a quarter.

## Deviations from the specs

- **The shelf is a list, not a flag.** A shelved build moves to
  `GameState.shelf`, so no in-flight read needed a filter (see above).
  `DevProgress.shelvedDay` still exists, as the decay's clock.
- **The declared v2 is a new action**, `startProductAsV2`. It is not a
  new argument on the `startProduct` case: `HouseField` pattern-matches
  that case's four fields, and a fifth would change a bots' file. The
  defaulted argument is on `ProductSystem.startProduct`, which is where
  the spec's "new defaulted argument" is honoured.
- **A one-time parent's units also take ×0.5.** The spec names
  "acquisition and churn", which only exist for subscriptions. Without
  this, a one-time product would have no old-version cost.
- **The J4 and shelve balance keys live in a new `build` block**, not in
  K2's `lifecycle` block, so K2's struct is untouched.
- **The tier clock is written only for a launch off standard.** A sheet
  launch at standard writes nothing, so it is byte-identical to `.ship`
  and keeps today's freedom to hold a sale in week one.
- **Scrap's crew morale lands on the people on the build now.** A shelved
  build has nobody on it, so a scrap from the drawer costs no morale; it
  paid its −3 on the way in.
- **Unshelving puts everyone idle onto the build**, the way `startProduct`
  and `startUpdate` do. The button says so.
- **The confirmation dialog became a sheet** on the product page and the
  list, with every answer kept. The war room's dialog (T7's
  `Screens/WarRoom/**`) still ships at standard, which is a follow-up.

## Follow-ups

- **The war room's ship button** (T7's `WarRoomScreen`) still sends plain
  `.ship`: no price, no replace, no run-both line. It should open
  `ShipSheet(engine:productID:)`.
- **Replacing loses to running both even with the decay** (J4's table): the
  40% carry is K2's number. If replacing should ever win inside a year,
  raise the carry or print the difference. That is an owner call.
- **A rough gate ship lowers the lineage** (P2's table, 110 → 69 on Round
  15), because `recordShip` sets the pools rather than keeping the better.
  This is invisible to the player. It is either a print for the ship sheet
  ("the Round 1 codebase drops from 110 to 69 points") or a
  `CodebaseSystem` change that keeps the better, which would move the
  pacing bots and so needs a re-pin round.
- **Names:** a shelved "Round 6" does not block a new "Round 6". S3's
  taken-name check reads `products` only.
- **The scrap founds a codebase with `productsShipped 0`.** The foundation
  step prints it as a lineage with nothing shipped. Honest, but not
  worded for it.
- **`-autoRoute t2-launch`** raises launch day by hand, as K2's
  `k2-launch` does: a send from the route bypasses the toast layer.
- `make strings` for the new copy (the coordinator's, after the merge).

## Debug flags

All take `-unlocked -autoFixture release-studio-day400 -autoTab products`
and leave the clock paused. If a question is waiting on the loaded save,
the route answers it with its first open option (as `-autoAnswer` would,
without starting the clock), so its sheet cannot cover the screen.

A trap worth recording for the next lane: the tools' shell is zsh, which
does not word-split an unquoted `$VAR`. A launch script that builds the
flags as `P="-unlocked -autoFixture …"` and passes `$P` to `simctl launch`
hands the app one argument it does not recognise. The app then opens the
simulator's own leftover save, silently. Use `${=P}` or an array. My first
two screenshot passes photographed the wrong game this way.
