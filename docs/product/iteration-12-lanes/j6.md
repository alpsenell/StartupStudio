# J6 — One queue, the children's clock, late-game money

*Iteration 12. Branch `j6-queue`, cut from `scaffold-12`. Merges first.*

Every question the game asks now arrives through one door with a deadline
on it. Children grow up before the campus. And a story's money scales
with the company it happens to, so a campus's story question no longer
costs less than a week's rent.

## What I built

### I5 — One queue for every question

**The engine**

- **`Queue.swift`** (new). `QueueBoard.entries(in:)` reads every open
  question out of the state into `QueueEntry`s. Each entry has a kind, a
  severity, a title, a category, the day it was raised, the last day an
  answer counts (`nil` when it waits for the founder), a one-line default
  and a surface (`.sheet` or `.room`).
  - Fourteen kinds: the seven that always had a sheet (story, poach,
    buyout, challenge, staff, notice, term sheet) and wave two's seven (the
    dirty-money offer, the string, the confrontation, the funeral, the
    case, the hearing, the old post).
  - Order: loud first, then the soonest deadline, then the oldest, then a
    fixed order of kinds.
  - **Derived, never stored.** The save carries nothing new, so identity
    holds by construction.
- **`QueueCap`**: two critical stops a week, and the third waits.
  - A stop is held only when every reason for it is critical *and* can
    wait. It can wait when the deadline answers it or when it waits for the
    founder: the story, the offers, the notice, the challenge, the
    dirty-money offer, the string, the case, the confrontation and the old
    post.
  - It never waits for money running out, a game over, somebody walking out
    or a room that has already opened.
- **The cap runs in `GameEngine.performTick`, on the live clock only.**
  - `Reducer.tick` and `PausePolicy.pausingEvents` grade the day exactly as
    before. So `economy.pauseEvents`, which is in the save and which
    `SimRunner` counts, stays byte-identical for every bot and fixture.
  - The engine remembers the week's critical stop days. They are not saved;
    a resumed engine starts paused anyway.
  - It publishes `queueHeldEvents` and `queueHoldCount` for the app.
- **The five direct `state.speed = .paused` writes are gone.** Each is
  replaced by a J6-marked note.
  - `FamilyDramaSystem:88` (`.familyAffairDiscovered`) and `:153`
    (`.familyParentDied`) raise tick events graded `.critical`, so
    `PausePolicy` already stops the clock on them. The rail and
    `lastPauseEvents` now say why. The old write left the clock "paused"
    with no reason, and the tick loop still running.
  - `CrimeSystem:709` (`.crimeHearingOpened`), `PrisonSystem:386`
    (`.insideParoleOpened`) and `FamilyDramaSystem:408` (`.familyDivorced`)
    are raised by the player's own action. The new
    `PausePolicy.roomPausingEvents(_:)` names them, and `GameEngine.send`
    stops the clock on them the way a tick does: the reason kept, the tick
    loop cancelled.
- `PausePolicy.pausingEvents` itself is untouched. PausePolicyTests pass
  unchanged; no pin moved.

**The app**

- **`DecisionPrompt.queueItems` and `queue`** (the J6 region of
  `DecisionSheet.swift`).
  - Each entry maps to its sheet through the existing builders; rooms have
    no sheet.
  - `pending(in:content:balance:)` keeps its signature and returns the
    queue's first sheet.
  - **A new kind of sheet goes in `queuePrompt(for:)`.**
- **"Let me think" works on every sheet but one.** The poach, buyout,
  staff, notice, term sheet, story, string and confrontation sheets can
  all be put off. Each has a deadline the engine answers for the founder,
  or waits for them, so putting it off is a real choice.
  - The exception is the category challenge, which stays modal.
    `RivalFightSnapshotTests` pins `isDeferrable == false` for it ("offers
    stay modal; the six weeks are the deadline"), and an app test is not
    mine to re-pin (Deviations, 6).
- **Wave two's two question sheets now reach the root**
  (`QueuePrompts.swift`).
  - **The string**: Comply / Stall / Refuse, with the same costs, heat and
    refusal reasons as `DirtyMoneyDemandSheet`.
  - **The confrontation**: the four `FamilyConfession` answers with their
    detail lines. It has no deadline, so the rail says "waiting on you".
  - The case, the hearing, the offer, the funeral and the old post are
    rooms. They sit on the rail with their deadline, and the rail's button
    walks the founder into the room.
- **The deferred set** lives in `GameShell`: `deferredQueueIDs`,
  `isDeferred(_:)`, `recall(promptID:)` and `holdForCap(_:)`.
  `deferredChoiceID` still exists and still means "the most recently put
  off" (the HUD's dot reads it).
- **The root presents the first question the founder has not put off.**
  - A deferred poach no longer hides the term sheet behind it, as the old
    fixed order did.
  - When the engine holds a stop, `.onChange(of: engine.queueHoldCount)`
    puts those questions straight on the rail.
- **`NoticeRail`** (J6 owns the file).
  - `RailNotice` gains `var answer: QueueRailAnswer? = nil`, and
    `RailNotice.queued(_:day:answer:)` is new.
  - `RailNotice.deferred(id:title:daysLeft:category:)` is unchanged. With
    no answer named, it still brings the deferred story beat back.
  - The rail shows one row per question, in the queue's order. That covers
    every sheet put off (while the clock runs) and every open room. Each row
    shows its real countdown, or "waiting on you" when `daysLeft` is
    negative.
  - The pause row's Details now routes wave two's stops: the offer → dirty
    money, the case → the ledger, the hearing → the courtroom, the parent
    and the divorce → the family, the cancellation → the feed.

**Seams for other lanes (J1 re-seats here at merge).** `NoticeRail` has
three named extension points. Each returns nothing today, so the rail
renders exactly as before, and each holds an empty `// MARK: J1 (doors)`
pair for J1's lines.

| Seam | Where it is read | What J1 puts there |
|---|---|---|
| `laneNotices(_ state:) -> [RailNotice]` | appended to `liveQueue` right after the queue's rows | the doors, as `RailNotice.deferred(id:title:daysLeft:category:)` rows for the current state |
| `fallbackTip: CoachTip?` | `activeTip` returns it when no active goal has a tip | `CoachTip.stateTip(in:dismissed:)` |
| `laneRoute(forNoticeID:) -> Route?` | the deferred row's button, for a row with no queue answer, before the original recall of the deferred story beat | `DoorRail.route(forNoticeID:)` |

The deferred row now carries its notice's id (`"deferred-<id>"`) for the
third seam.

### I2 — The children's clock

- **`ChildhoodBalance.stageDays`: `[180, 540, 1100, 1800]` →
  `[90, 270, 540, 900]`.** The block is a computed default, not JSON, so the
  change is one line. Ages are still read off the stage.
- **The `kid_` audit.** There are eleven `kid_` life events.
  - Only two roll on their own (`kid_drawing`, `kid_sick_night`), gated
    only on `requiresChildren`.
  - The other nine are `followUpOnly`. Four of them are stage beats —
    `kid_first_sentence`, `kid_first_day`, `kid_teen_door`,
    `kid_moves_out` — which `ChildhoodSystem.advanceStages` schedules on
    the day the stage turns. They follow `stageDays` with no JSON edit.
  - No `kid_` event gates on an absolute day or an age in years. The intern
    summer and disowning read `Child.stage(on:)`.
- **What that means for a run.** A child born around day 200 is now a
  toddler at day 290, at school at 470, a teen at 740 and grown at 1,100.
  - A 730-day run reaches school age.
  - The teen door, the intern summer and moving out now land in the first
    months of year three, the first year past the pacing horizon, where
    they used to land in year four and year six.
- **The pinned suites did not move.** `ProgressionBots` does send
  `.haveChild`, so "no bot has a child" was not quite true. Even so,
  ProgressionTests and InvestorTargets' four-year independent run stayed
  green, and no childhood unit test pins a stage day.

### I3 — Late-game money has teeth

- **`BalanceConfig+Queue.swift`** holds `QueuePacingBalance`: one block with
  both keys off at the Swift default. `"queuePacing"` is appended at the end
  of `Balance.json` with them on:
  - `eventCashReferenceBurn 6000` (the brief asked for 4000; see the
    board-gate check below);
  - `campusRentBase 4000`, `campusRentPerHead 200`,
    `campusRentFreeHeadcount 14`.
- **Event stakes.** `NarrativeSystem.apply` multiplies each `.cash` effect
  by `max(1, burn / 6000)^0.5`, rounded to $10.
  - Burn is `GameState.queueWeeklyBurn`, the same sum as
    `GameEngine.weeklyBurn`.
  - At exactly 1.0 the amount is untouched and not even rounded. The garage
    and the loft, up to $6,000 a week, never reach the multiplication.
  - A studio at about $11k a week reads ×1.35. The median unflagged story
    cost goes from $2,500 to about $3,385. A campus at about $40k reads
    ×2.58, about $6,450.
  - **The button agrees with the ledger.** `NarrativeChoicePresenter`
    scales each option's `cashDelta` the same way and rewrites the written
    figure in the option's detail line ("−$1,200" → "−$1,490"). Above 1× it
    adds a "Stakes ×1.2" stat.
  - `.loan` effects and life-wallet effects are not scaled.
- **The campus.** `officeWeeklyRent` reads `queueCampusRent`:
  `$4,000 + $200 × max(0, headcount − 14)`. It is scaled by the
  difficulty's office factor, as the listed rent is, then by the district
  and the Operations discount as before. That is $4,000 with 14 people and
  $9,200 at 40.
  - **Buy and sell price.** `officePurchasePrice` is `buyPriceFactor`
    weeks of the tier's *listed* `weeklyRent`, so it does derive from rent.
    I left the listed $12,000 alone, so the deeds cost what they did. The
    $300k `upgradeCost` is unchanged too.
  - **Campus column.** It reads 0 for every BalanceTargets bot, at
    scaffold and now: no seed reaches the campus inside 730 days, so the
    campus-no-earlier-than-day-500 gate is unaffected. InvestorTargets'
    funded bots do reach the campus, and that is where this key shows.

### The board gate: the check the lead asked for

The gate compares the rate of met board reviews over three years: the
founder who plays to the board's number (`InvestorBot`) against the one
who ignores it (`.ignoresTheBoard`). Neither bot ever answers a story, so
every story takes its default at the deadline. Stakes can therefore only
bite harder on whichever bot runs the higher burn.

I instrumented `QueueStakes.scaled` in a scratch copy (never committed)
to count the extra cash stakes took from each bot.

| Variant (campus key on unless said) | Pleasing: met rate | Ignoring: met rate | Gate as written (`>`) | Pleasing: extra stakes cost, mean multiplier | Ignoring: extra stakes cost, mean multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `scaffold-12` (both keys off) | 61/89 = 0.685 | 55/81 = 0.679 | passes by 0.006 | — | — |
| Stakes only, reference $4,000, campus off | 59/92 = 0.641 | 57/80 = 0.713 | fails | — | — |
| Campus only, stakes off | 62/90 = 0.689 | 58/82 = 0.707 | fails | — | — |
| The brief: reference $4,000, ^0.5 | 60/91 = 0.659 | 57/84 = 0.679 | fails | −$42,850, ×1.84 | −$144,190, ×2.20 |
| Reference $4,000, ^0.4 | 66/90 = 0.733 | 59/82 = 0.720 | passes by 0.013 | −$53,810, ×1.70 | −$95,440, ×1.80 |
| **Shipped: reference $6,000, ^0.5** | **65/91 = 0.714** | **53/80 = 0.663** | **passes by 0.051** | −$39,200, ×1.63 | −$79,280, ×1.73 |

"Ousted" read 0 against 0 in every run, including the scaffold's.

**Cause.** The hypothesis was that stakes hits the board-pleasing bot
harder. It does the opposite: the ignoring bot pays two to three times
more, because it keeps hiring and runs the higher burn. The flip at $4,000
was not a tax on playing to the board. It was path noise on a gate that
held by 0.006 at scaffold, and each key alone was enough to move it.

**Why $6,000.** Both gentler defaults pass the gate as written. I picked
the $6,000 reference because:

- it passes by 0.051, not by the single review the ^0.4 variant passes by;
- it needs no new key, where the ^0.4 variant needs an exponent field;
- the studio's median story still costs about $3,385 (×1.35), a clear 35%
  over the old $2,500, against $3,745 for ^0.4 and $4,146 for the brief.

## How it fails — the checks I ran

- **Identity.** With both keys on, every fixture that stays under the
  reference burn is byte-identical: the day-30 fixtures,
  FullLoopDeterminism, the legacy saves and `release-garage-day40`.
- **Which key moves which gate.** Covered by the board-gate table above:
  the scaffold, each key alone, the brief, and both gentler variants.
- **The cap never touches a bot.** `SimRunner` counts
  `state.economy.pauseEvents`, which the cap does not write. Any change in
  `pauses/y` comes from the stakes key changing the run, not from the cap.
- **The queue on a real save** (screenshot 1). On the day-40 fixture with
  `-autoQueue` at x1, the story and the confrontation both went to the
  rail. The story led with its real deadline, the confrontation waited
  behind it, and the clock ran. On a resumed save, a poach carried "Let me
  think" (screenshot 2).
- **The button agrees with the ledger** (screenshot 3). At the campus with a
  $5,000 founder salary, the burn is about $9,250, so ×1.24. The patent
  letter's "−$1,200 · it ends today" read "−$1,490 · it ends today", with
  "−$1,490 → $1,010" under it.
- **A weak spot I saw.** At x4, a five-day story deadline passes in about
  five seconds of wall time, so a player at 4× who puts a question off has
  very little real time. The rail's red "1 day left" is the only warning.
  At x4 the queue shot's story had already answered itself; at x1 the
  countdown shows. That is the deadline's design, not the queue's, but the
  queue makes it more visible.

## Deviations

1. **The cap lives in `GameEngine`, not in `PausePolicy`.** The policy's
   output is in the save and is what the bots count, so capping there would
   move every fixture. On the live clock the result is the same.
2. **`QueueEntry` is shaped like a `PendingChoice` but is not one.** It has
   an id, a title, a deadline, a default and a severity. Wave two's
   questions keep their own state, and the queue reads it. Moving each
   system's pending state into one stored queue would have changed four
   lanes' save formats for no player-visible gain.
3. **The case, the hearing, the offer, the funeral and the old post stay
   rooms.** Their answers are multi-step (a lawyer, then a defence, then
   the exchanges) or already have a room built for them. The rail carries
   them with their deadline and walks the founder in.
4. **`pending(in:content:balance:)`'s body is now the queue's first
   sheet.** A lane that inserted a sheet into the old fixed chain will
   conflict at merge. The new home for a sheet is `queuePrompt(for:)`.
5. **Touches outside the scaffold's markers**, each at the point of use
   with a J6 comment:
   - `GameEngine.swift`: the cap and the room stop;
   - `GameShell` and `AppRootView`: the deferred set, the root's pick, the
     hold and the debug hook;
   - `NarrativeChoicePresenter`: the button's money line.
6. **The category challenge keeps no "Let me think".**
   `RivalFightSnapshotTests.testTheChallengeIsThePendingDecisionAndCarriesTheNumbers`
   asserts it stays modal. Making it deferrable is one line in
   `queuePrompt(for:)` plus flipping that assertion, which is for the owner
   or the PM to allow.
7. **The event-stakes reference ships at $6,000, not the brief's $4,000.**
   At $4,000 the board gate fails on path noise. At $6,000 it passes as
   written, and the studio's story money still has teeth (×1.35 against
   the brief's ×1.66). Setting the key back to 4000 in `Balance.json` gives
   the brief's numbers exactly, at the cost of re-pinning that gate.

## Follow-ups

1. **J1's doors.** J1's six `NoticeRail` lines go in the three seams above,
   between the J1 markers already there: the doors in `laneNotices(_:)`,
   the state tip in `fallbackTip`, and the door route in
   `laneRoute(forNoticeID:)`. `RailNotice.deferred(id:title:daysLeft:category:)`
   is unchanged. Longer term, a door could be a `QueueKind` with an entry
   in `QueueBoard.entries`, so it sorts with every other question.
2. **J3's price war** is a new sheet. It needs a `QueueKind`, an entry in
   `QueueBoard.entries`, and a builder in `DecisionPrompt.queuePrompt(for:)`.
3. **A real countdown at 4×.** Consider holding the tick for one wall
   second when a deferred question reaches its last day. A design
   question, not a bug.
4. **Two sheets now duplicate the root's.** `FamilyConfrontationSheet` and
   `DirtyMoneyDemandSheet` still live inside their rooms, so they are a
   second way to the same answer. A later pass could have the rooms open

5. **The funeral, the case and the old post could become sheets** once

6. **InvestorTargets' doc comment on the board gate** still describes
   ousting numbers from iteration 6 (2 against 3). They have read 0

## Debug flags

- `-autoQueue [eventID]` — a story (`patent_troll` unless named) in
  tomorrow's post and the partner waiting up. For the next minute, every
  question is put off onto the rail as it arrives.
- `-autoChild baby|toddler|school|teen|grown` — a child a week into that
  stage.
- `-autoCampus` — the company on the campus.
- `-autoStakes [eventID]` — the campus, a founder at $5,000 a week, and a
  story with money in it in tomorrow's post.

All four go through `.queueDebugSeed(kind:value:)`, which the reducer
applies in debug builds only.
