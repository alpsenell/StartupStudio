# J3 — Rivals and the market

*Iteration 12. Branch `j3-rivals`, cut from `scaffold-12`.*

Three joins between rivals and the market. First, rivals now read the
market: a boom brings a studio in, a crash empties the topic, launches lean
toward demand, and the forecast finally has a line that differs by topic.
Second, a price war asks a question instead of just stopping the clock.
Third, the card a copycat copies finally counts for something: it is worth
half on your board, it makes their clone better, it shows as a chip in your
hand, and reviewers notice.

## What I built

### The engine

- **`RivalMarket.swift`** (new) defines the state and the pure reads:
  - The state types: `RivalMarketState`, `RivalMarketMove`, `RivalMarketWarAnswer`.
  - The answer and its refusals: `RivalMarketPriceWarAnswer`, `RivalMarketPriceWarRefusal` (six reasons, each a sentence).
  - `RivalMarketCircling`.
  - The public reads on `enum RivalMarket`:
    - `productTypeID(for:content:)`
    - `circling(topicID:state:)` and `isBesieged`
    - `copiedBy(cardName:topicID:state:)`
    - `pendingPriceWar` and `answerDeadline`
    - `refusal(answering:…)`, a dry run of the real handler on a copy
    - `warProduct`
- **`Systems/RivalSystem+RivalMarket.swift`** (new) holds the rival system's half:
  - the launch-topic pick;
  - the product type;
  - the weekly boom recruit and crash drop (`rivalMarketMoves`);
  - the three answers (`rivalMarketAnswer`);
  - the daily out-ship and end-of-war pass (`rivalMarketDaily`);
  - the weekly match bleed;
  - the copied card (`rivalMarketCopy`);
  - the DEBUG-only seeds.
- **`Balance/BalanceConfig+RivalMarket.swift`** + `"rivalMarket"` appended at the end of `Balance.json`:
  - 11 keys, each decode-if-present against the shipped default.
  - The top-level key is optional via the `KeyedDecodingContainer` overload.
- **`Systems/RivalSystem.swift`**, nine J3 marked regions:
  - the weekly moves before evolution;
  - the weighted pick on the `pick(rival.focusTopicIDs, &state.worldRNG)` line;
  - the product type in `launchProduct`;
  - the worst card and the copy call in `copycatCheck` (below M1's lines; J5's delay line goes at the top, untouched);
  - "a matched war takes no share" in `recomputeShare`;
  - the match bleed after `bleedStrength`;
  - the daily pass before `mirrorShareIntoMarket`;
  - one line in W3's `espionageFalsePlans`;
  - an internal wrapper around the private share pass.
- **`FeatureBoard.swift`** (J3 markers): `FeatureCardReading.copiedBy` (defaulted, so the memberwise init is unchanged). The copied card's fit term is multiplied by `copiedFitFactor`, and the verdict says so.
- **`Market.swift`** (J3 markers): `MarketForecast.rivalMarketLine(topicID:state:balance:)` gives the forecast its first topic-specific line.
- **`Reviews.swift` / `ProductSystem.swift`** (J3 markers): `ReviewContext.bestFeatureCopied`, and a `featureCopied` callout placed just before `featureLed`.
- **`GameAction`**: `.noticeMarketOpened`, `.answerPriceWar(rivalID:answer:)`, and `.seedRivalMarket(scenario:)` (DEBUG only).
- **`Reducer`**: the three handlers.

  - four `GameEvent` cases (`rivalMarketEntered`, `rivalMarketLeft`, `priceWarAnswered`, `priceWarOutshipped`), all at the default `.info` severity;
  - the `rivalMarket` slot, decode-if-present and encoded only when it is not `.empty`.

### Rivals follow the money

Everything below waits for `state.rivalMarket.noticed`. Only
`.noticeMarketOpened` sets it, and the app sends that the first time the
Market segment appears (`MarketView`). No bot ever sends it.

| Rule | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| Launch topic | Still one `worldRNG` word. Before the market is opened it is `nextInt`'s word, exactly as before. After, it is `nextUniform`'s word mapped through weights of multiplier^2 in focus order: ×1.4 → 1.96, ×0.6 → 0.36. |
| Boom recruit | The week a topic's history crosses `featureBoard.boomAppetiteThreshold` (1.35), read off the last two history samples, the strongest studio above strength 40 moves in. It must not be the incumbent, not a ghost, and not already there. No draw. |
| Crash drop | A focus topic whose last 4 weekly samples are all under ×0.70 is dropped. Every studio keeps one: if all its topics crashed, it keeps the least-bad. The incumbent and ghosts are exempt. |
| Product type | The argmax of the topic's `fitByType` (a missing entry reads 1.0), with catalog order breaking ties. Fitness makes a mobile app, Finance an enterprise tool, and so on. |
| The forecast line | "Northwind moved in on the boom 2 weeks ago and has not shipped yet. At ×1.46 it draws 2.1× the launches of a quiet market." Other variants: crash exits, "followed the boom here", copycat, home. |
| Map | A boom entrant that hasn't shipped raises the siege marker. Once the market is opened the legend reads "Under challenge, or a rival moving in", and the spoken summary says "a rival moving in". |
| Paper | Entries and exits are `.market` journal events, so the market column prints them. One J3 line names a studio circling a category you hold. |
| Profile | "Where they are going": each focus topic with its multiplier and why the studio is there (the boom and its date, where they started, followed you, took it in a fight), whether they are selling, recent crash exits, and the price war with your answer. |

### Answer the price war

The war now opens a decision sheet (`RivalFight/PriceWarPrompt.swift`),
reached from one J3 hook in `DecisionPrompt.pending`, after the category
challenge. The prompt is built on the app side from the rival's
`priceWarUntilDay`; the engine keeps no pending question. The answer window
is the war's first week, 7 days. After that the war is outlasted, which is
today's behaviour.

| Answer | What it does | On the button |
|---|---|---|
| Match them | The best live product in the topic goes budget and returns to its old tier when the war ends (unless you re-priced it since). The −0.10 share penalty is lifted for this war. The rival loses 1.5 strength a week and gains 25 grudge. Raises `rivalmarket_matched`. | "goes budget for 4 weeks · the −10% lifts · Marrow −1.5 strength a week · grudge 0 → 25", plus "past 45 they start working against you" when it crosses. |
| Out-ship them | Starts a patch on the best product, or adopts one already underway. A patch landing on or after the war's first day ends the war that day and gives +8 standing in the topic. | "lands by day 428 and the war ends, standing +8", or the refusal "Every build slot is busy. A patch needs one." |
| Outlast them | Records the answer. Nothing else changes. | "−10% of your Finance share until day 428 · nothing else moves" |

**The espionage join, checked.** Two Matches against one studio take its
grudge from 0 to 50, past `espionage.rivalGrudgeToAct` (45). That makes it
`OfficeSecretsSystem.angryRival`, the studio N5's slow-burn machine runs the
three W3 rival operations for. But that machine returns on its first line
until `state.secrets.watching` is set, and only the Team tab sets that. So
for a founder who has opened the Team tab, Match really does start the
mole, the tail and the hack. For one who never has, **Match costs only the
margin**, which is the "if a rival at 45+ rarely acts, Match is free" risk
the spec warned about. It is follow-up 1; changing N5's gate is not my file.

### The copycat takes the card

All of this waits for `RivalProduct.copiedFeature`, which only a board with
a card on it can write. No bot places a card.

- While the clone competes (`relevanceWeeks`, 26), the copied card's fit term on your products in that topic is ×0.5. A natural fit drops from 1.0 to 0.5.
- The clone gets +4 quality, clamped to 95. Raises `rivalmarket_copied`.
- A studio fed W3's false plans lifts your **worst** card (the lowest value on the board) on its next clone. The flag is then cleared.
- Hand rows show a "copied by <studio>" chip and an explanation before you place the card. Placed cards show a "copied" badge, and the verdict reads "Northwind shipped this first. Everyone has it now; it counts for half."
- Reviews: `bestFeatureCopied` fires the new `featureCopied` callout ("Everyone has {feature} now. This one has it too."), using the existing `{feature}` token and the word already drawn. No new draw.

### Content

- **Three `rivalmarket_` events**, appended at the end of `Events.json`. Each is gated on a flag only J3 raises:
  - `rivalmarket_boom_company` (`rivalmarket_company`, raised when a studio moves into a boom you sell in)
  - `rivalmarket_matched_margin` (`rivalmarket_matched`)
  - `rivalmarket_copied_card` (`rivalmarket_copied`)
- **One review callout**, `featureCopied`, with five lines in `Reviews.json`.
- **`EventCopy`** (J3 markers): the four journal lines. Entries and exits file under Market, answers under Rivals.

## How it fails — the check I ran

**The spec's check:** a product launched into a boom should out-earn one
launched into a quiet topic by 15–30% over 26 weeks. Losing is wrong, and
so is winning by 80%.

**How I ran it.** A scratch Swift package in the session scratchpad, not a
test and not in the repo:
- It copies `SimRunner.swift` and `PacingBots.swift` out of the engine's test target and drives SoloSlow through the public `Reducer`.
- Four rivals, the ten `BalanceTargetsTests` seeds.
- On the day the bot first ships, the state forks into four worlds. The product's topic is either ×1.42 and crossing the boom line (last sample ×1.20), or a quiet ×1.00. Each of those runs with F3 off and with F3 on.
- Each world runs 26 more weeks. I sum that one product's first 26 weeks of revenue.

**Round 1, as specced.**

| | mean | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F3 off, boom / quiet | 1.403 | 1.390 | 1.143 | 1.621 |
| F3 on, boom / quiet | 1.376 | 1.424 | 0.817 | 1.633 |

The instrumented run says why F3 barely moved it:

- **Is the recruit firing?** On 7 of 10 seeds. On the other 3, no studio was above strength 40 when the product shipped (the bot's out-selling bleeds them).
- **Does the entrant ship into the boom?** Rarely. The strongest studio is recruited into *every* boom on the board, so it reaches 3–7 focus topics (seed 999331: seven). A ×1.42 topic weighted against that many others, times a 10% weekly ship chance, is 3–5% a week. Only 2 of 7 entrants reached the shelf inside the 26 weeks.

**Retune: an entrant ships where it moved in.** The PM's "at a 10% weekly
ship chance it arrives in about a quarter" assumes the entrant's next
launch goes to the boom, so that became the rule. While a studio's boom
entry is newer than its last launch, and it has nothing on sale there, its
next launch goes to that topic. The single `worldRNG` word is still drawn,
so the stream does not move, and it is still behind `noticed`.

**Round 2, with the retune (shipped).**

| | mean | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F3 off, boom / quiet | 1.403 | 1.390 | 1.143 | 1.621 |
| F3 on, boom / quiet | 1.380 | 1.413 | 0.827 | 1.633 |

**Result: the check fails the band, from above.** A boom launch still
out-earns a quiet one by about 40%, with F3 on or off. It never reaches
80%, and it loses on 1 seed of 10. Three things keep the boom's premium
intact, and none of them is a J3 key:

1. **The copycat arrives in both worlds.** Week 8 on 8 of 10 seeds (`copycatDelayWeeks`), so both worlds lose the same share to it.
2. **Entrants are weak.** A studio of strength 40–55 ships quality 34–47 (`qualityPerStrength` 0.85) against SoloSlow's ~70, so an arrival takes little share: 0.64 → 0.59 on seed 4242.
3. **Sales are front-loaded.** Arrivals land at weeks 4–14, on the tail of the product's adoption curve.

The one loss (seed 123457, 0.83) is the entrant the design describes: quality 55 in week 4, taking the boom share from 0.74 to 0.48.

**What this means for the decision the spec wanted.** "Never enter a
boom" has *not* become the answer, which was the failure the spec feared.
But "always enter a boom" is still the answer, with a slightly larger
variance. Getting into the band needs one of these, and each is a
design call, so each is follow-up 6 rather than something I invented:

- a funded entrant (a quality bonus on its first launch into the boom);
- two entrants per crossing;
- a lower `entrantMinStrength` together with the first.

The harness is ready to re-measure any of them.

**Round 3, a funded entrant (tried at the lead's request, not shipped).**
A studio recruited by a boom had its first launch there floored at the
topic's leader minus a gap. The leader is the best live review score, or
the best competing rival product, in that topic. The floor was clamped to
the rival quality range and sat behind `noticed`, with the gap as a
`rivalMarket` key. Same ten seeds; the F3-off rows are unchanged
(1.403 / 1.390).

| Variant | mean | median | min | max | seeds losing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped (no floor) | 1.380 | 1.413 | 0.827 | 1.633 | 1 of 10 |
| Floor = leader − 8 | 1.377 | 1.412 | 0.827 | 1.633 | 1 of 10 |
| Floor = leader − 4 | 1.376 | 1.412 | 0.827 | 1.633 | 1 of 10 |
| Entrant ship chance 25% (round 4) | 1.376 | 1.414 | 0.865 | 1.639 | 1 of 10 |

**Round 4, a hurried entrant (tried at the lead's request, not shipped).**
While a studio holds a boom entry it has not shipped into, its weekly
launch roll used a threshold of 25% instead of 10%. It was the same single
uniform roll with the threshold rescaled, behind `noticed`, as a
`rivalMarket` key.

- **It did bring arrivals forward.** On 64064 the entrant shipped in week 7 and that seed fell from 1.43 to 1.29. On 999331 it shipped in week 20.
- **It did not move the mean.** Entrants still reached the boom inside 26 weeks on only 3 seeds of 10. On the other seven there was either no studio above strength 40 at the crossing, or the recruited studio never launched into the boom topic in the window.
- **The losing seed is unchanged.** 123457 still loses (0.86), with its entrant shipping at quality 53 in week 4.

The code is back at the shipped state, and the key is not in the tree.
Four rounds now agree: with this bot and this rival field, the premium a
boom carries is set by *whether* a strong studio exists to answer it, not
by how fast or how well it answers.

**Neither variant lands in the band.** The code is back at the shipped
state; the floor is not in the tree. The measurement shows why quality
was not the limiter:

- **Arrival.** Over the 26 weeks the entrant reaches the boom on only 3 of 10 seeds (4242, 55055, 123457). On the other 7 there is either no studio above strength 40 at the crossing, or an entrant that does not ship inside the window at a 10% weekly chance.
- **The floor barely bites.** It moves exactly one seed: 4242 goes 1.40 → 1.38 → 1.36.
- **The losing seed was already strong.** On 123457 the entrant shipped at 55, which is above both floors, so it was untouched.

A better-funded entrant cannot move a boom premium it never reaches.
The lever that would move it is *when*: a higher ship chance for a
studio holding a pending boom entry, or recruiting at the crossing a
studio that already has a product in the wings. Either changes the ship
roll's odds, not its draw count, so it could stay behind `noticed` with
no new draw. That is follow-up 6.

## Identity

- `noticed` is the only gate on F3, and nothing but the app sets it.
  - The pick uses `nextInt`'s one word until then.
  - `rivalMarketMoves` returns on its first line.
  - `rivalMarketProductType` returns `nil`, which keeps `content.productTypes.first`.
- `rivalMarketDaily` and `rivalMarketMatchBleed` return at once while no war has been answered.
- `isMatched` is false for every war nobody answered.
- The copied card needs `copiedFeature`, and no bot places a card.
- `RivalMarketState` encodes only set fields, and the slot is written only when it is not `.empty`.
- New events are gated on J3 flags.
- The pacing suite (`rivalCount = 0`), the investor suite (`rivalCount = 4`) and the four fixtures did not move: 943 of 943 engine tests pass with no pin changed.

## Deviations

1. **The sheet is modal, not deferrable.** A deferrable prompt goes to the notice rail with a countdown, which is J6's file this round. The engine does default an unanswered war to Outlast at the end of the week, so the war is a natural candidate for the rail once J6's one-queue lands (follow-up 2).
2. **TopicDetailView was not edited.** Its line 312 labels the *player's* products. Rival products get no type label anywhere in the app today: the shelf on the profile shows the topic icon. The mobile-app bug is fixed in the data (`RivalProduct.typeID` is now the topic's best type once the market is opened). Showing it is follow-up 3.
3. **No new fields on `Rival`.** The answers, the product to restore and the false-plans flag all live in `RivalMarketState`, so a rival that folds mid-war still gives the product its price back, and `Rival.swift` is untouched.
4. **Rival products already on a shelf keep the type they were saved with.** The new type applies to launches after the market is opened. Retyping old products would move saved bytes.
5. **`-autoMarketReport`** is a DEBUG-only flag in my own file, because no `-autoRoute` name reaches the report sheet where the categories card lives. It is read in MarketView's J3 region.
6. **A studio that moved in on a boom ships there first.** This is not
   in the spec's bullet list. It is the spec's own arithmetic ("at a 10%
   weekly ship chance it arrives in about a quarter"), which only holds if
   the entrant's next launch goes to the boom. Without the rule, the
   entrant collected every boom on the board and rarely reached the shelf.
   The one `worldRNG` word is still drawn, and the rule sits behind
   `noticed`. Measured in both forms above.

## Follow-ups

1. **Make Match cost an enemy even without the Team tab.** `OfficeSecretsSystem.run` stands down until `secrets.watching`. Either the angry-rival threads should start without it, or Match's grudge needs a second, visible consequence (N5's file).
2. **The price war on the notice rail** with its seven-day countdown, once J6's queue can take a prompt whose engine default exists.
3. **Show rival product types**: a type icon or name on `RivalShelfCard` rows and the head-to-head panel.
4. **Rivals re-entering a recovered topic.** A studio that walked out of a crash comes back only on a new boom crossing. A slower drift back at ×1.0 may read more naturally.
5. **The forecast line outside held categories.** It shows on every category row once the market is opened, but the forecast itself only shows at standing 50+. The design question is whether "who is circling" should be what standing buys.
6. **Get the boom premium into 15–30%.** Measured at about +38% with F3
   on (see above). A funded entrant was tried at leader − 8 and leader − 4
   and moved nothing, because the entrant reaches the boom inside 26 weeks
   on only 3 seeds of 10. The next lever is arrival: a higher ship chance
   while a studio holds a pending boom entry, or two entrants per
   crossing. Both change the odds of the existing roll, not the number of
   draws. The scratch harness forks every seed at first ship and
   re-measures a variant in about two minutes.

## Debug flags

- `-autoRivalMarket boom` — the newest product's topic booms (×1.46, crossing the line), its forecast becomes readable, and the strongest studio moves in.
- `-autoRivalMarket crash` — the first studio's home market sits under ×0.70 for a month and it walks out.
- `-autoPriceWar` — a studio starts a price war in your newest released product's topic (conjuring one on a fresh save). The sheet comes up.
- `-autoCopied` — a clone lifts the best unplaced card in the newest build's hand (use with `-autoRoute featureboard`).
- `-autoMarketReport` — opens the market report sheet as the market appears.
- `-autoRivalMarketCard` — lifts the profile's "Where they are going" card onto a sheet. It sits below the fold, and a headless pass cannot scroll; this is W3's `-autoSpyCard` pattern.

All are sent through `.noticeMarketOpened` plus one `.seedRivalMarket` from
`RivalMarketDebug`, which is started in the app root's task. Release
builds read none of them.
