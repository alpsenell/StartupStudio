# M3 — The incident room

*Iteration 10, lane M3. Branch `m3-incident`, cut from `scaffold-10`.*

## What was built

When a live product breaks, the clock stops and a full-screen room opens.
Three lanes — **mitigate**, **communicate**, **fix** — and the whole payroll
to put on them; an hour button that says what the hour will buy before it is
spent; a pixel status page that goes red → amber → green as the work lands;
a count of the users walking out while you argue; and three statements, one
of which becomes the week's front page.

Every mutation is a `GameAction`, the room's own hour hand included, so an
incident is deterministic and replays from the action log. Nothing reads a
wall clock and nothing draws from `rng` or `worldRNG`.

### Engine

- **`Incident.swift`** (M3's file, reshaped): `IncidentKind` (three kinds),
  `IncidentThread` (three lanes), `IncidentStatus` (red/amber/green),
  `IncidentStatement` + `IncidentStatements` (three per kind, written per
  product name), `IncidentState` (the room: assignments, three progress
  values, users lost, statement, hours spent, the reach it started with),
  `IncidentLog` (the run's two facts) and `IncidentOutcome`.
  `IncidentState` and `IncidentLog` are hand-Codable: the assignment and
  cooldown maps encode as arrays sorted by UUID, and every key decodes with
  a default.
- **`Systems/IncidentSystem.swift`** (new): raising, the four actions, and
  the settlement.
- **`Balance/BalanceConfig+Incidents.swift`** (new) plus the `"incidents"`
  object appended to `Resources/Balance.json` after `"sabbatical"`.
- **`EconomyState.incidents: IncidentLog`** in a marked M3 region of
  `Economy.swift` — field, coding key, decode-if-present, and
  **encode-only-when-non-empty**, so a run that never opens the Products
  tab writes exactly the bytes it wrote before this lane existed.
- Marked regions used as given: `GameAction` (five cases), `GameState`
  (`GameEvent`'s two cases, plus the two arms added to the `severity`
  switch, labelled `// MARK: Iteration 10 — M3`), `Reducer` (systems list —
  deliberately empty, with the reason; handlers — the five actions), and
  `LiveOpsSystem.run` (a new `// MARK: Iteration 10 — M3` region at the end
  that calls `IncidentSystem.raiseIfNeeded`).

### App

- `App/Sources/Screens/WarRoom/Incident/IncidentRoomScreen.swift` — the

- `.../Incident/IncidentNowRow.swift` — the Now card's row, rendered only

- `.../Incident/IncidentFixture.swift` — `-autoIncident` and its debug

Every type this lane declares is prefixed `Incident`.

## The kinds, their triggers and their outcomes

All three are checked in `IncidentSystem.raiseIfNeeded`, at the end of
`LiveOpsSystem.run`, behind one gate:

```
balance.incidents.enabled
&& state.economy.incidents.hasOpenedProducts   // the app's flag
&& state.incident == nil                       // one at a time
&& state.gameOver == nil
```

and, per product, `state.day - lastIncidentDay[product] >= cooldownDays`
(91 — one a quarter). At most one incident is raised per tick, and the two
deterministic causes are checked in `state.products` order before the roll.

| Kind | Trigger | Room's shape |
|---|---|---|
| **`badPatch`** | A patch landed within `badPatchWindowDays` (3) *and* the release's `liveBugs` ≥ `economy.liveBugAlarmThreshold × badPatchBugFactor` (12 × 1.0). Deterministic — no draw. | Bleeds 1.6% of reach an hour; a botched repair leaves up to 14 live bugs; −6 reputation unmitigated. |
| **`viralSpike`** | This week's units ≥ `spikeGrowthFactor` (1.8) × last week's, units ≥ `minimumReach` (120), and `ProductSystem.weeklyHostingCost` ≥ `spikeHostingShare` (0.35) of the week's revenue. Deterministic. | 1.1% an hour; up to 6 bugs; −4 reputation. |
| **`dataLeak`** | Once a week (`day % 7 == 0`), on the biggest on-market release with reach ≥ 120: one `state.socialRNG.nextUniform()` against `leakWeeklyChance` (0.02), multiplied by `leakLegalFactor` (0.25) when the studio has a **Legal** department. The lane's only draw, and it is inside the gate. | 2.4% an hour; up to 9 bugs; −11 reputation. |

**Reach** is `subscribers` for a subscription release and last week's units
otherwise, captured once when the incident is raised so the bleed does not
chase the number it is changing.

**One hour** (`.advanceIncident`): everybody assigned advances their lane by
`pointsPerPersonHour` (9) × a skill factor (`skillFloor` 0.6 +
`skillSpan` 0.9 × skill/100, where skill is coding for *fix*, marketing for
*communicate*, the mean of coding and design for *mitigate*), + `roleBonus`
(0.45) for the obvious role, × the founder's or the employee's own output
multiplier. Then the hour's churn lands:
`reach × kind.bleedShare × (1 − relief)`, where relief is
`mitigate/100 × 0.6 + communicate/100 × 0.3`, capped at 0.9. Eight hours
(`hoursPerIncident`) and the day is gone.

**Resolving** (`.resolveIncident`, refused with a reason until a statement
is chosen):

- **Users.** `usersLost × statement.churnFactor` comes off `subscribers`
  for a subscription; for a one-off release it comes off `liveHype` at
  `hypePerUserLost` (0.02 a head) — a one-off has no subscriber count to
  churn, so the audience leaving shows up as the word of mouth going.
- **Bugs.** A finished repair halves `liveBugs`; an unfinished one adds
  `kind.bugLoad × (1 − fix/100)`.
- **Reputation.** `−kind.reputationHit × (1 − mitigate/100)`, plus
  `statement.reputationDelta × credibility` where credibility is
  `credibilityFloor` (0.4) + 0.6 × communicate/100, minus
  `brokenPromisePenalty` (6) if the statement promised a fix that never
  shipped.
- **The world.** `.incidentRaised` is `.critical` (it always stops the
  clock, which is what opens the room); `.incidentResolved` is `.notable`
  and lands in the `.company` strand, so the newspaper composer leads the
  week with it for free. The office thread posts when the room opens and
  when it closes (only once somebody other than the founder is on the
  payroll — L1's rule), and the partner texts on the outcome.

**Statements**, three per kind, always the same three shapes: *own it*
(best churn, promises a fix), *explain it* (safe), *downplay it* (protects
nothing and costs reputation). Every button carries its own consequence —
`+2 reputation · 30% fewer leave · promises a fix you have not shipped` —
and the promise line turns warning-coloured while the fix is unfinished.

## Identity at the default

The gate is the whole story: `IncidentLog.hasOpenedProducts` is set by
exactly one action, `.noticeProductsOpened`, dispatched by `AppRootView`
when `router.tab` becomes `.products`. A pacing bot, a fixture replay, a
headless test and `ReleaseFixtureGenerator` never switch tabs, so
`raiseIfNeeded` returns on its first line, no balance value is read, and
the `socialRNG` draw is never taken. `EconomyState` encodes `incidents`
only when it is not `.empty`, so an untouched run's save is byte-identical.
The engine's 943 tests and the committed release fixtures pass unchanged.

## Deviations

- **"Never in a run with `rules` that forbid it."** No such rule exists on
  `GameRules`, and `RunMode.swift` is M4's file, so nothing was added. The
  gate above already excludes every non-player run; a `rules.incidentsOff`
  flag is a one-line follow-up for whoever owns `RunMode`.
- **Assignment is two taps, not a drag.** Tap a lane to arm it, then tap
  people. A drag *also* works (`.draggable` / `.dropDestination` on the
  lanes), but the tap flow is the one the room is designed around: a drag
  onto a 44-point lane, over the room's own scroll view, loses more taps
  than it wins on a phone.
- **The room can be put aside.** The brief has the room opening itself and
  the clock stopped. It does — but the ✕ closes the cover without closing
  the incident (the clock stays stopped, and the Now card's row brings the
  room back). A modal with no way out and a required choice underneath it
  is a trap; this keeps the pressure without the trap.
- **`.assignToIncident` / `.chooseIncidentStatement` / `.advanceIncident`
  emit no events**, so `GameEngine.send` does not autosave between hours.
  Only `.resolveIncident` does. Killing the app mid-room resumes at the
  last saved hour rather than the current one.
- **`-autoIncident` runs on its own engine**, the way `-autoRoute warRoom`
  does: a headless launch has no shipped product to break, so the fixture
  plays one (`IncidentFixture`) and the room is shown on that engine. The
  consequence is that the *outcome* — the journal line, the front page —
  cannot be photographed headlessly, because closing the room drops back
  to the session's own game. Closing does work: `-autoIncidentClose`
  dismisses the cover, which only happens when `state.incident` becomes
  `nil`.
- **Re-pinned one expectation.** `StringsAuditTests.pixelTextLiteralBaseline`
  **47 → 48**. The room's two prose pixel strings went through
  `String(localized:comment:)`; the one bare literal left is
  `PixelText(text: "\(incident.usersLost)")`, a pure number, which is what
  the baseline counts. No test was added.

## Debug flags

- `-autoIncident <badPatch|viralSpike|dataLeak>` — plays a fixture company
  to a live release with three people around it, raises that incident and
  opens the room.
- `-autoIncidentPlay` — puts the three of them on the three lanes, works
  three hours and picks the first statement.
- `-autoIncidentScroll` — scrolls the room to the statement panel.
- `-autoIncidentClose` — resolves it three seconds later.
- `-autoRoute incident` / `incidentroom` — the `.incidentRoom` route.

## Follow-ups

- A `rules` switch for incidents (M4 owns `RunMode.swift`).
- The office scene is not in the room. The war room draws
  `OfficeSceneView`; a red-lit version of it, with the people on lanes at
  their desks, is the obvious next thing and belongs to whoever next owns
  `OfficeFX`.
- An incident's outcome does not reach `LegacyLedger`, so "the night we
  nearly lost it" is not in the biography.
- The room does not autosave between hours (see Deviations).
- Support desks and the incident do not know about each other: somebody on
  a support desk is just another portrait on the bench.
- No incident has ever been raised in a *played* run inside this lane's
  verification — the triggers are unit-shaped and deterministic, but they
  have only been exercised through the fixture, not observed firing in a
  long session. Worth a play-through at merge.
