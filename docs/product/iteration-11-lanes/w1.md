# W1 — Dirty money

*Iteration 11, wave two. Branch `w1-dirtymoney`, cut from `scaffold-11b`.
Simulator `ws-l1`.*

## What it is

When the bank has stopped answering and the term sheets have dried up,
somebody else calls. Three of them, each with a different cheque and a
different set of strings, and every string has a date on it. Say yes to
enough of them and the money is fine; say no, or say nothing, and things
start happening to the office, the car and the people who know you. Every
dollar that goes through them is a line on the founder's own record with
an offence attached, which the auditor can find and N1's courtroom can
hear.

## The three backers

Cheques scale with the weekly payroll (capped at 3×) so the figure is
always a plausible amount of money for the company it lands in. The
figures below are the base, before that scaling.

| Backer | Cheque | The strings | What they cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kasimov Family Office** | $240,000 | *A consultant* on the payroll from month two (day 56); *a market of their choosing* by month six (day 168) | Anton is 0.8% of the cheque a week and is never in the office; the market is a topic they name off the catalog, not one you picked |
| **Meridian Partners** (the front) | $90,000 | *An invoice* from day 45, then every 90 days; *the nephew* 30 days after the first invoice is paid | The invoice is 12% of the cheque (floor $4,000) and is laundering; the nephew is 0.5% a week |
| **Denny** (the shark) | $30,000 | The **vig**, weekly, taken in `FinanceSystem`'s W1 region; *a visit* when a week could not be paid | 3.5% of the cheque a week (floor $500); the visit costs three weeks' vig and has a two-day clock |

An offer only appears when the company is in trouble — cash under four
weeks of operating cost, **or** a term sheet declined inside the last
quarter (read out of `eventLog` for `.investmentDeclined`, so
`InvestorSystem` was not touched) — and not before day 30. It stands a
week, one `socialRNG` word a week decides whether it comes, and each
backer is offered at most once a run.

## Answering a string

Comply, stall or refuse, each with its price and its heat on the button
before it is pressed, and a refusal that says why in place of the price.

- **Comply** pays what it says (company cash first, wallet for the
  remainder) and cools the heat by 6.
- **Stall** buys 14 days, once per string, and costs 9 heat (11 on a
  payment: payments weigh 1.25×).
- **Refuse** costs 26 heat (33 on a payment).
- **Saying nothing** is a refusal the deadline gives for you.

## Heat and what it pays out as

Heat is 0…100, cools 1.5 a week while nothing is outstanding, and above 25
rolls weekly for a reprisal (chance rises with the heat, ceiling 40%, with
a 21-day cooldown so a bad month is not a bad week). What lands depends on
what there is to lose:

- **the window** — $3,500 of glazing on the ledger and 7 morale off
  everybody;
- **the car** — N3's `AssetsState.owned` loses the car and
  `HomeDecor.remove` takes it off the drive; it posts N3's own
  `.assetStolen`, because from the founder's side that is what happened.
  Nothing in `AssetsSystem` was edited: N3's report says removing the
  entry is the whole of making a thing stop existing, and it is;
- **a friend** — the strongest bond loses 22 points and sends one text;
- **a rival** — +6 strength, from a fund with your fund's address.

## Laundering: the seventh offence

`CrimeOffence.launderMoney` is added in W1-marked regions of `Crime.swift`
(nine switches: the case itself, the six copy properties, the gravity, the
notoriety and the discovery rate) and written in exactly one place —
`CrimeSystem.dirtyMoneyLaunder`, W1's marked region in `CrimeSystem.swift`.
It writes an ordinary `CrimeRecordEntry`, so the weekly sweep rolls for it
as it rolls for the other six, the case it raises is heard in the same
room, and `CrimeSystem.confess` turns the founder witness on it. The
cheque itself, every invoice, every vig payment and the payoff are all
laundering.

Two seams were bent rather than followed, and both are commented in place:

- `Crime.notorietyCost` returns **0** for the seventh offence, because the
  notoriety of a payment is a fraction of the payment and W1's own balance
  block owns it (`launderNotoriety`, 7 a payment). Nothing is added twice:
  the offence is never committed through `CrimeSystem.commit`, which
  refuses it for every caller.
- `Crime.discoveryChance` reads `Crime.launderDiscovery`, a constant
  (0.035 weekly) rather than a balance key, because that function is
  handed `BalanceConfig.CrimeBalance` alone and a seventh field in N1's
  block is not W1's to add. Noted as a follow-up below.

## The three ways out

- **Pay them off** — the cheque × 2.2, plus 1.4× a surcharge for the heat.
  The payment is itself laundering, which is the joke.
- **Turn witness** — `CrimeSystem.confess` on the newest open laundering
  entry, so the case is listed today rather than in some week a roll
  chooses. The `money_witness` flag stands for the rest of the run and
  puts a floor of 55 under the heat, so the reprisals never stop. W4's
  sentence, if the room hands one down, is the same sentence N1 already
  writes.
- **Sell up to them** — 45% of the valuation (never less than the cheque),
  35% of it into the founder's wallet, and the run ends `.soldUp` with
  their name in the reason line.

## Where it is in the app

`DirtyMoneyCard` is the **first card** in the Business tab's Finances
section, above the run rate and the bank loan — an approach has a week on
it and a string has a date on it, and the two things you read underneath
(the runway, the credit limit) are why you are on this page. It draws
nothing at all until somebody has called. Three sheets:
`DirtyMoneyOfferSheet` (pixel paper, the strings written out with the
figures the engine will actually use, and the laundering warning in red
above the button), `DirtyMoneyDemandSheet` (the string, its clock, and the
three answers), `DirtyMoneyExitSheet` (three doors, priced). A one-line
`DirtyMoneyApproachNote` sits under the term sheet on Investors and links
across, because a founder who has just turned a round down is on that page
and not this one.

## Identity at the default

`state.dirtyMoney` is `.empty` until `.noticeFinancesOpened`, which
`FinancesView.onAppear` sends — the `noticeProductsOpened` pattern.
`DirtyMoneySystem.run` returns on its first line while it is empty, and
`BalanceConfig+DirtyMoney` is read from nowhere else. No pacing bot opens
a screen, so no bot is ever offered a cheque; the byte-identical fixture
tests pass unchanged. Draws are `socialRNG` only: one word a week for the
offer roll plus one to pick the backer, one a week for the reprisal roll
plus one to pick which. `rng` and `worldRNG` are untouched.

## Strings

Every new string is in Swift (no `make strings` catalogue was touched by
this lane beyond the ordinary sweep the PM runs once): the backers'
names, blurbs, pitches and strings lines and the five demands' bodies live
on the enums in `DirtyMoney.swift`; the reprisal lines on
`DirtyMoneyReprisal`; the phone posts and ledger labels in
`DirtyMoneySystem` and `FinanceSystem`'s W1 region; the journal, feed and
newspaper copy in `EventCopy.swift`'s W1 region (eight events, with four
small helpers beside N5's).

## Events

**Thirty-eight**, all prefixed `money_`, appended at the end of the two
catalogs:

- `Events.json` 147 → **171** (24 new), gated on `money_backed` (their
  money is in the account), `money_history` (it ever was) or
  `money_witness`. Six carry choices with deadlines; `money_bank_letter`'s
  passive answer schedules `money_bank_second_letter`, a `followUpOnly`
  second act 40 days later.
- `LifeEvents.json` 208 → **222** (14 new). `money_partner_asks` is the
  one that matters: lie and `money_partner_found_out` arrives 50 days
  later with the affection cost tripled. Two of the fourteen fire only
  once the money is *gone*, so paying them off has a sound.

## Balance

`"dirtyMoney"`, 45 keys, appended after `"assets"` in `Balance.json`, with
an inline default in `BalanceConfig` so a balance file without the object
is the game that ships.

## Debug flags

- `-autoRoute dirtymoney` (also `backer`, `facility`) — Business tab,
  Finances segment.
- `-autoDirtyMoney <backer>` — puts an offer on the table (`familyOffice`,
  `theFront`, `theShark`; bare takes the front) and stops the clock, since
  an offer stands a week and a week at x4 is gone before a headless pass
  can photograph it.
- `-autoDirtyMoneyTake` — bank the cheque and run on to the first string.
- `-autoDirtyMoneyDemand <kind>` — bank the cheque and pull one string
  today (`consultant`, `theirMarket`, `invoice`, `nephew`, `lateVisit`).
- `-autoDirtyMoneyOut` — bank the cheque and open the three ways out.

Only the offer and the demand's *date* are seeded, the way `-autoSecret`
seeds a thread's stage; taking the money and answering are real
`GameAction`s through the ordinary reducer.

## Deviations, and what was left out

1. **The seventh offence's discovery rate is a constant, not a balance
   key.** `Crime.discoveryChance` takes `BalanceConfig.CrimeBalance` and
   only that, and adding a field to N1's block is out of W1's ownership.
   The one-line fix for whoever owns `BalanceConfig+Crime.swift` next is a
   `launderMoneyDiscovery` field there; `Crime.launderDiscovery` is the
   single place to change.
2. **"Hire my nephew" is a passenger, not an `Employee`.** Both the
   consultant and the nephew are `DirtyMoneyPassenger`s: a name, a title
   and a weekly cost charged as payroll, with a morale drip on everybody
   who is actually doing the work. They hold no desk and appear in no org
   chart, which is the joke, but it also means `HiringSystem` was not
   touched (it is not W1's file). A follow-up could mint a real employee
   through N1's `crimeMintPoachedCandidate`-shaped seam.
3. **"A product that must ship into a market they name"** records the
   topic (`namedTopicID`) and complying is a promise rather than an
   enforced build; nothing checks later whether you shipped into it. The
   state is there for whoever wants to add the check.
4. **The demand sheets are SwiftUI sheets, not `EventDef` story sheets.**
   The brief allowed either; a real sheet driven by engine state can print
   the heat delta and the refusal on the button, which JSON choices
   cannot. The consequence is that a string does not appear on the
   `DecisionPrompt` notice rail — it stops the clock through its
   `.critical` event and opens itself when the player is on the page, and
   the card carries the clock otherwise. `DecisionPrompt` is a shared file
   with no W1 region; a one-line hook there is the follow-up.
5. **No screenshot of the laundering entry on N1's crime ledger.** The
   lane's debug pass starts from `FinancesView.onAppear`, so a headless
   run cannot both bank a cheque and land on the Life tab's ledger. The
   entry is verified in the engine (the record, the notoriety and the
   `1` on the way-out sheet's "payments still open on the record" row in
   `04-way-out.png`), but there is no picture of the courtroom hearing a
   laundering charge.
6. **Reprisals never touch the property or the pet**, only the car. Two of
   four reprisals need something to exist (a car, a friend); the window is
   the fallback and always available.
