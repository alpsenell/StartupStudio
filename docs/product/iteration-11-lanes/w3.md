# W3 — Espionage

*Iteration 11, wave two. Branch `w3-espionage`, cut from `scaffold-11b`.*

Do to a rival what the mole is doing to you: a private investigator on
their founder, somebody of yours inside their office, the poach that the
folder makes possible, their roadmap bought off a person who had a copy,
and their storefront down in the week they launch. Every one of them goes
on N1's record as **industrial espionage**, which means the weekly crime
sweep rolls on it for the rest of the run and the courtroom can hear it.
Rivals run the same playbook back: three new `SecretKind`s on N5's
slow-burn machine, answered by three new `SecretResponse`s — sweep the
office, audit the roster, feed them false plans.

## What I built

### The engine

- **`Espionage.swift`** — `EspionageOperation` (the five, with pitch,
  record line and icon), `EspionageDossier` (three facts and a leverage
  that goes cold after `dossierColdDays`), `EspionageIntel` (what they are
  shipping, its codename and the day), `EspionageOpRecord`,
  `EspionageRefusal` (ten reasons, each a sentence), `EspionageState`
  (decode-if-present, encode-when-non-default, `.empty`), and the pure
  `Espionage` half: `cost`, `notorietyCost`, `successChance`,
  `traceChance`, `grudge`.
- **`Systems/EspionageSystem.swift`** — `refusal`, `operate` (the one
  place money moves, the record entry is written and the two rolls
  happen), the payoffs, and a daily `run` that closes a mole's report on
  the day it named. Returns on its first line while `state.espionage` is
  `.empty`. Draws: `socialRNG` only — one uniform for whether it lands,
  one for the trace, one for which three facts the folder has, one for the
  codename. `rng` and `worldRNG` are never touched.
- **`Balance/BalanceConfig+Espionage.swift`** + `"espionage"` appended
  after `"assets"` in `Balance.json` (43 keys, every one decode-if-present
  against the shipped default).
- **`Systems/RivalSystem.swift`**, W3 marked region: `espionageGrudge`,
  `espionageCoolGrudge`, `espionageLostTheirBest`,
  `espionageRoadmapWalked`, `espionageHackStorefront`,
  `espionageIntercepted`, `espionageFalsePlans` — every write to a studio
  lives in the file that owns studios.
- **`Crime.swift` / `Systems/CrimeSystem.swift`**, W3 marked regions: a
  seventh `CrimeOffence`, `corporateEspionage` (gravity 0.95 — the
  gravest of them), with an arm in all ten switches; a `CrimeRefusal`
  case `elsewhere` so the ledger's row says *"Not from here. This one
  starts on a studio's own page, with a name on it."*; and
  `CrimeSystem.raiseCase(against:)` opened from `private` to internal
  (visibility only — nothing else about it moved), which is exactly the
  seam N1's report promised.
- **`GameAction`** `.runEspionageOperation(operation:rivalID:)`,
  **`Reducer`** (system + handler), **`GameEvent`** nine cases in the W3
  region with a severity block (only `.espionageTraced` stops the clock).

### The rival's half, on N5's machine

- Three new `SecretKind`s in a W3 region of `OfficeSecrets.swift`:
  `rivalMole`, `rivalTail`, `rivalHack`, with `isRivalRun`, a `spy_`
  arm in `eventID` (so `OfficeSecretsSystem.scheduledEventIDs` covers
  them without a second list) and `confrontDefID` pointing at W3's own
  staff defs.
- `canStart` for all three is `angryRival(state, balance)` — the studio
  with the highest `Rival.grudge` past `rivalGrudgeToAct` (45). Grudge
  only ever gets there because the founder did something first (N2's
  taunts, W3's own operations), so a run that never touches a rival never
  sees one, on top of N5's own `watching` gate.
- Casts, three stages of clues each (with the room's props and a phone
  message), and endings: their mole ships your roadmap through N5's own
  `leakRoadmap`; their investigator hands an open record entry to
  somebody who prints, which raises a case; their contractor takes a week
  of live hype and adds live bugs.

### The counterintelligence menu

Three new `SecretResponse`s, refused on any thread the office is running
itself (`SecretRefusal.notRivalRun`), each with its cost on the button:

| Answer | Cost | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Sweep the office | $3,500 of the company's | Ends the thread `.handled`; the studio running it loses 20 grudge |
| Audit the roster | An evening | Names them; on their mole, walks them out (`.departed`) |
| Feed them false plans | Free, needs them named, mole only | Ends it `.dealt`; the studio's focus becomes the weakest-demand topic on the board and they lose 9 strength |

- `EventCopy` W3 region: nine journal lines, all filed under **Rivals**.
- `SecretsCard`: the three counterintelligence rows get their cost line,
  their confirmation title and their confirmation body under W3 markers.
  No structural change — the card already lists `SecretResponse.allCases`.

### Content — 33 `spy_` events, 6 `spy_` staff defs

- **21 thread beats**, `followUpOnly`, three stages plus four endings for
  each rival-run kind (`spy_rivalmole_s1` … `spy_rivalhack_faced`). They
  are reachable through `OfficeSecretsSystem.scheduledEventIDs`, which
  builds from `SecretKind.allCases`, so the content suite accepts them
  with no test edited.
- **12 flag-gated company events** behind `spy_record` / `spy_traced` —
  the invoice Finance would like a word about, the investigator who wants
  to buy you dinner, the founder you have had followed shaking your hand,
  the burner phone in your drawer, the trade desk asking on the record.
  Gated exactly the way N1's `crime_` events are, so a clean run's event
  pool is the pool it always was.
- **6 staff defs**: three confrontations (`spy_confront_rivalmole`,
  `…rivaltail`, `…rivalhack`) and three second acts. The weekly staff
  roll only ever considers defs whose id *is* a `StaffEventKind` raw
  value, so none of these can be drawn; they are raised by `defID` and by
  follow-up.

## The five operations

| Operation | Cost | Odds it lands | Odds it is traced | What it buys |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Put somebody on their founder | $3,000, the founder's wallet | 72% base | 10% | A dossier: three facts, leverage 1.0, +0.22 on every later operation against them for 120 days |
| Place a mole | $9,000, the company | 58% | 16% | Their next launch, 28 days early: topic, codename, date. Be on that shelf first and the launch lands with −12 quality |
| Poach with the dirt | $5,000, the founder's wallet; needs a dossier | 55% | 20% | Their best engineer in your candidate pool; the studio −5 strength |
| Buy their roadmap | $16,000, the company; needs a build in flight | 50% | 24% | +22 hype on your build, the studio −6 strength, the topic on your stolen list |
| Take the storefront down | $12,000, the company; needs something of theirs on sale | 46% | 32% | 55% off their newest product's weekly units, −7 their reputation |

Success is `base + marketKnowledge/260 − strength/100 × 0.28 + dossier
leverage × 0.22`, clamped to 12…90%. The trace roll is the operation's
base warmed by `notoriety × crime.notorietyDiscoveryFactor`, halved by a
Legal department, and **×1.8 when the operation botched** — a botched
operation still costs the money and is far easier to follow. Notoriety
added: 6 / 10 / 12 / 14 / 20. A traced operation takes the rival's grudge
to 100 and raises a case through `CrimeSystem.raiseCase`; an untraced one
still earns 18 grudge and still leaves a record entry the weekly sweep
rolls on at `plantStoryDiscovery`'s rate for the rest of the run.
Cooldown: 21 days between operations, whoever they are against.

## Deviations

1. **The dossier does not touch the pitch room's warmth.** The brief asks
   for "raises your pitch warmth against them"; `PitchRoom` is M2's file
   and I have no marker in it. The leverage instead adds to the odds of
   every other operation against that studio and gates the dirty poach.
   Follow-up below.
2. **The feature board's "they are shipping X" card is exposed, not
   drawn.** `EspionageState.liveIntel` is the read M1's board wants; the
   card that draws it lives on the rival's profile rather than on
   `FeatureBoard.swift`, which is M1's.
3. **A seventh `CrimeOffence` rather than a private record.** It gives the
   courtroom, the settlement, the confession and the weekly sweep for
   free. It also puts a seventh row on N1's ledger, which refuses itself
   with `CrimeRefusal.elsewhere` and points at the rival's page — I could
   not filter the row without editing N1's screen. Expect a both-added
   merge with W1's `laundering` in the same ten switches.
4. **The espionage offence's weekly discovery base is
   `crime.plantStoryDiscovery`** rather than a number of its own:
   `Crime.discoveryChance` takes `CrimeBalance`, which is N1's file, so a
   new field there was not mine to add. The per-operation odds are in
   `balance.espionage` and are the ones rolled at the moment of the act.
5. **`-autoSpyCard`.** The card sits below the fold of a long profile and
   a headless pass cannot scroll (N1 hit this and left it as follow-up 3).
   A DEBUG-only flag lifts the same card onto a sheet, entirely inside my
   own file; nothing in the game presents it.
6. **The last counterintelligence row is below the fold** in
   `04-counterintelligence.png`, for the same reason. The Team tab's
   scroll view is N5's.
7. **The rival threads reuse `StaffEventKind.officeMole`** for their
   confrontation (their *defs* are W3's own). New kinds live in
   `Social.swift`, which is N5's file, and the moment is still a
   conversation with somebody on your own payroll.
