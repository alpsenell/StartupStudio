# W2 — Family drama

*Iteration 11, wave two. Branch `w2-family`, cut from `scaffold-11b`.*

## What I built

**Discovery.** `FamilyDramaSystem` reads N2's `affairContactID` /
`affairSinceDay` and rolls once a week on `socialRNG`. Discovery sets
`affairDiscoveredDay` (through a new `InteractionsState.markAffairDiscovered`
seam, so the field is written from exactly one place), posts to the partner's
phone thread, raises `family_affair_found`, pauses the clock and opens a
confrontation with four answers — confess, deny, end it, pack a bag. Rule 11
is kept: the affair is a fact and this is a conversation about a fact.

**Divorce.** `InteractionSystem.breakUp` runs first — N2's deliberate end of
a relationship, which leaves the home, the children and the things alone —
and then W2 divides what it left. The settlement sheet is two columns with
everything on the table; the running total is `GameState.assetSplitValue`,
which is the arithmetic the engine is about to do, so the number on the sheet
is the number in the save. A long marriage costs a slice of the company off
`equityRemaining`, held the way `Origin`'s co-founder holds theirs — not a
round, no board seat, no ask; `GameState.exPartnerEquity` names it.

**Custody.** A `LegalCase` with `kind == "custody"` and `isFounderSuing`,
listed four to eight weeks out, heard in N1's courtroom with a family opener,
family replies and its own verdict bands. The evidence is the children's
memory ledgers. The verdict moves every child's bond and, through
`FamilyCustody.keepsTheHouse`, decides who keeps the roof in the settlement.

**The people onboarding never made.** Two parents, a sibling and — while
there is a partner — the partner's parents, derived from `state.seed` the way
`FriendRoster.derive` derives the friends. Nothing about a name or a face
costs a byte; only what the founder *changed* (`FamilyKinRecord`) is saved.
The parents age towards a care bill and then a funeral (a stopped day with one
argument to settle); the sibling asks for a job, then a stake, then a loan;
the in-laws have a spare room that saves rent and costs mood.

**The will.** A sheet naming partner / a child / the longest-serving / the
sibling / nobody. It is recorded on `LegacyRun` (`willHeir`, `willHeirName`,
`willHeirChildID`) and `Successors.offers` puts the named person first with
"Named in <founder>'s will." appended to their relation line.

**The calendar.** `FamilyCalendar` gained the parents' birthdays (dated beats
that recur a year on and, unlike the partner's dates, survive a breakup) and
`family_anniversary_after` — the anniversary you now dread, swapped in by
`anniversaryVariant` while the affair is on the record.

**Content.** 39 `family_` life events appended at the end of
`LifeEvents.json`, including the three dated ones.

## The identity gate

`FamilyDramaSystem.run` returns on its first line unless
`state.interactions.hasAffair || state.familyDrama != .empty`. Neither
happens without the player: an affair is N2's interaction, and
`familyDrama` first becomes non-empty when `.openFamilyRoom` is sent by the
room's `onAppear`.

Adding events to `LifeEvents.json` is *not* free: an ungated life event joins
the pool the weekly roll draws from and moves the byte-identical fixtures. It
did, once — seven of my beats had no `flagsAll` and `OriginTests`,
`ReleaseFixtureGenerator`, `Origins`, `Two ladders` and the balance-target
suites all went red. Every `family_` beat is now behind a flag only this lane
raises (`family_room`, `family_affair_found`, `family_divorced`,
`family_care`, `family_bereaved`, `family_will`) or is `followUpOnly`, and
the fixtures are byte-identical again. **Worth writing down for W1, W3 and
W4: a gate on every new life event, or the fixtures move.**

## The discovery odds

`FamilyDrama.discoveryChance(weeksRunning:intervened:fame:balance:)`, rolled
once a week on `socialRNG` while `affairIsSecret` and the founder is not
single:

```
chance = min(ceiling, base × (1 + weeks × ageFactor) + intervention + fame)
```

| Term | Key | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Base weekly | `discoveryWeekly` | 0.07 |
| Per week running | `discoveryAgeFactor` | 0.06 (of the base) |
| A standing vice intervention (N3's `intervened`) | `interventionDiscoveryBonus` | +0.05 |
| Fame, at 100 (N4) | `fameDiscoveryBonus` | +0.12 |
| Ceiling | `discoveryCeiling` | 0.42 |

So a fresh affair is about 7% a week; at three months it is about 12%; with a
partner already counting the evenings and a founder who is photographed at
lunch it reaches the low thirties. Nothing is ever certain in one week, and
the median secret lasts roughly two months.

Discovery costs `discoveryAffectionHit` (30) of affection on the spot, and
the answer costs more: confess −22, deny −38, end it −14, leave ends it.

## The settlement rules

1. **`InteractionSystem.breakUp` first.** Stage to `.single`, the partner
   cleared, affection to zero, the diary's partner dates removed,
   `people_estranged` raised. It touches nothing else, by design.
2. **The founder's share** is
   `FamilyDrama.entitlement(lawyer:theirLawyer:affairDiscovered:marriedDays:)`:
   half, plus `(myLawyer.weight − theirLawyer.weight) × 0.55`, minus 0.10 if
   the affair is on the record, then pulled back towards half by up to half
   the remaining distance over eight years of marriage, and clamped to
   0.28…0.72. Their solicitor is one `socialRNG` draw: usually a tier below
   yours (55%), sometimes the same (35%), occasionally better (10%).
3. **The things** are dragged (or tapped) into two columns. Anything the
   founder keeps above their share is settled in cash out of the wallet;
   anything below comes back. Everything not kept is removed from
   `assets.owned`, which takes the weekly bill, the mood drift, the net worth
   and — through `HomeDecor.remove(slot: HomeDecor.assetSlotID(for:))` — the
   driveway with it.
4. **The home follows the children.** With children, `FamilyCustody
   .keepsTheHouse` decides and the column is ignored; with none, the column
   decides. Losing it is one rung down the ladder (the same move an eviction
   makes, so L7's decor follows the tier) and is paid back in cash at
   `balance.life.home(tier).upgradeCost`.
5. **The pet by name.** `AssetOwned.petName` goes whichever way it is dragged,
   and the settlement records the name.
6. **The wallet** (N3's crypto) is money, not a thing: half the units, always.
7. **The company** is untouched under `equityMarriedDays` (728 days). Past it,
   `3 + 1.5 × years` points, capped at 12 and at half of what is left, come
   off `equityRemaining`. `GameState.exPartnerEquity` returns the name and
   the points for a cap table.
8. **The in-laws** were the partner's; their records are dropped.
9. Solicitors: duty (free), high street ($4,000), the one on the building
   ($18,000) — `CrimeLawyer`'s three tiers, priced for a family court.

## The custody evidence table

`FamilyDrama.custodyStanding` averages every child's ledger, adds the bond
above default, adds the lawyer, subtracts a known affair, and clamps to
−100…100:

```
standing = (Σ memoryPoints + Σ (bond − 50) × 0.3) / children × 4
         + lawyer.weight × 40
         − (affairDiscovered ? 18 : 0)
```

| Memory kind | Points |
|---|---|
| `birthdayKept` | **+3** |
| `evening` | +2 |
| `internSummer` | +2 |
| `wedding` | +1 |
| `sabbatical` | +1 |
| `launch` | +0.5 |
| `chapter`, `exit`, `grewUp` | 0 |
| `internQuit` | −1 |
| `hospital` | −1.5 |
| `burnout` | −2 |
| `eviction` | −2 |
| `missedBirthday` | **−4** |

Verdict bands on the standing after the three exchanges: **full** ≥ 34,
**shared** −6…34, **weekends** −34…−6, **none** below −34. Bond deltas: full
+6, shared 0, weekends −9, none −22. The opening standing is mapped onto
N1's 0…1 `evidence` scale (`0.5 − standing / 200`) so the courtroom's
existing grading of an exchange reads it unchanged.

## Deviations

1. **`App/Sources/Dynasty/Successors.swift` is not in my ownership table**,
   but the brief points at it for the will and no other wave-two lane claims
   it. I added one marked `// MARK: Iteration 11, wave two — W2` region (a
   call to `FamilyWillSuccessors.namedFirst`) and turned `Successor.relation`
   from `let` to `var` so the will can append its sentence. Flagged for the
   PM.
2. **One existing test learned three ids.** `ContentVarietyTests`'s
   "every follow-up resolves, and follow-up-only events are reachable" unions
   the ids a system schedules itself (`ChildhoodSystem.stageBeatIDs`,
   `OfficeSecretsSystem.scheduledEventIDs`); my three `followUpOnly` dated
   beats needed the same line, so it now also unions
   `FamilyCalendar.familyDramaScheduledEventIDs`. No test was added and no
   count changed (943 before, 943 after).
3. **Two content-test requirements shaped the copy.** Every dated beat's
   polite miss must cost affection ≤ −20, so missing a parent's birthday
   costs the partner's affection too (they watched you do it); and every
   choice-bearing event needs a `body`, which four of mine gained.
4. **`Systems/LifeSystem.swift` is not mine**, so the funeral's three days
   away are written straight onto `life.awayUntilDay` / `awaySinceDay` /
   `awayReason` rather than through a helper there.
5. **The in-laws' "spare room" is a room action, not an event chain.** The
   brief's "the in-laws have opinions and a spare room" is one toggle with a
   weekly saving, a mood drift and an affection drift, plus four `family_`
   events. A slower arc would want its own thread.
6. **The sibling's stake and loan are one-shot**, not a ladder of repeated
   asks; the escalation is job → stake → loan and stops.
