# L4 — Friends with names

Branch `l4-friends`, cut from `scaffold-9`. Three people from before the
company: names, faces, a bond that decays, a life of their own, and three
ways a long friendship can turn into something the company feels.

## What shipped

**Engine**

- `Friends.swift` (rewritten, L4 owns it): `FriendArchetype` (`uniFriend`,
  `exColleague`, `neighbour`, each with a blurb and a buyout opinion),
  `Friend` (bond, `metDay`, `lastContactDay`, `lastSeenDay`, `lastCallDay`,
  `companyName`/`companyValuation`, `movedAwayDay`, `hiredDay`, `loan`),
  `FriendLoan`, `FriendTuning` (every number the feature uses),
  `FriendsState` (roster + `seenEventStamps` + `opinedBuyoutDay`), and
  `FriendRoster.derive` — see *The one structural deviation* below.
  Hand-written `Codable` on `Friend` and `FriendsState`: every field
  decodes as its default, and `FriendsState` encodes nothing while empty.
- `Systems/FriendSystem.swift` (new): daily decay, the life-event hooks,
  the buyout opinion, the weekend, and the six actions.
- Actions in the `// MARK: L4` regions of `GameAction.swift` and both L4
  regions of `Reducer.swift`: `.callFriend`, `.seeFriend`, `.hireFriend`,
  `.investInFriend(amount:)`, `.borrowFromFriend(amount:)`, `.repayFriend`.
- `LifeSystem.swift`: the `.friends` weekend resolves through
  `FriendSystem.spendWeekend`, inside `// MARK: Iteration 9 — L4`.
- Public read API on `GameState` so the app quotes the engine's own gates
  rather than copying them: `friendRoster(content:)`, `friend(_:content:)`,
  `friendTuning`, `lastLine(from:)` and six `friend…Blocker` functions.

**Content**

- `Names.json`: a `friendNames` pool of 30, appended; `NamePools.friendNames`
  decodes-if-present with an empty default, and the roster falls back to
  `firstNames` when it is empty, so an old catalog still loads.
- `LifeEvents.json`: four beats appended after the last existing entry —
  `friend_wedding_invite`, `friend_started_something`,
  `friend_hit_the_wall`, `friend_leaving_town`. **Each requires the
  narrative flag `friends_in_your_life`**, so they only enter the life
  roll for a run that has actually engaged with a friend (see below). L3
  appends its `kid_*` entries before mine in merge order; expect a trivial
  both-added merge at the end of the array.

**Hooks on the six existing beats.** `friends_wedding`,
`friend_moves_away`, `friend_startup_advisor`, `burnout_friend`,
`holiday_alone` and `old_friend_visits` now name somebody. The system
learns a beat fired by watching `state.narrative.cooldowns[id]` change
(`NarrativeSystem.markFired` stamps it at fire time), which keeps the hook
inside my own files — no edit to `NarrativeSystem`. Each beat picks the
archetype it belongs to, moves that friend's bond, and posts a line to
`state.life.phone.post(…, from: .friend(id), …)`. `friend_moves_away` /
`friend_leaving_town` freeze the weakest bond permanently and keep the
thread; `holiday_alone` names who is not there.

**The buyout.** While `state.rivals.pendingBuyout` is on the table, the
closest friend at bond ≥ 70 texts their archetype's opinion, once per
offer. Text only — no number moves.

**App** (`App/Sources/Screens/Life/Friends/`)

- `FriendsCard` under Family: three portraits, the bond as a small bar,
  the last thing each said, and a footer that names whoever has heard
  least from you.
- `FriendsScreen` (`Route.friends`, `-autoRoute friends`): the `friends`
  vignette from `ActivitySceneComposer` with the neglected friend as the
  companion, the evening pips, the three of them as rows, what the weekend
  would do, and a debt note when you owe them money.
- `FriendSheet`: header (portrait, archetype, blurb, bond, days since you
  spoke), the last line on pixel paper, *Your time* (ring / an evening)
  and *What this could become* (join you / back their company / a loan),
  plus a *What you owe them* card with the repayment. Every row carries
  its consequence (`−1 evening · −$60 → $1,880 · bond +9 · relationships
  +6`) and, when shut, the engine's own reason (`They'd have to trust you
  more (60/70)`).

## The one structural deviation, and why

The doc says "generate lazily on day 1 in `FriendSystem.run` from
`socialRNG`". Both halves of that break rule 1 and I did neither:

1. **Not `socialRNG`.** Generating on day 1 would advance the shared social
   stream for every run, moving every downstream networking and
   relationship roll — the pinned balance would shift for runs that never
   open the Life tab. The roster comes from a private stream derived from
   `state.seed` (`seed &* 0xB5AD…DA1CE2A9 &+ 4`), exactly the way
   `worldRNG`, `investorRNG` and `socialRNG` are themselves derived in
   `GameState.newGame`. Nothing else in the lane draws at all: a friend's
   company name, its valuation and their skill sheet are arithmetic on
   `appearanceSeed`.
2. **Not written to state on day 1.** `OriginTests`'
   `aGarageIsByteIdenticalToTheScaffold` and the release fixtures pin the
   bytes of a played run, and three friends on day 1 broke all four. So the
   roster is **derived, not stored**: `FriendRoster.derive(seed:names:day:)`
   is a pure function that also prices in the silence since day one, and
   `GameState.friendRoster(content:)` returns it while
   `state.life.friends` is empty. The card and the screen therefore show
   three named, fading friends from day one with **zero bytes on the
   wire**. `FriendSystem.materialise` writes the roster down the first time
   the founder *does* something (call, evening, hire, invest, borrow) and
   raises `friends_in_your_life`; from then on the stored roster is the
   truth and the daily system runs.
3. **The `.friends` weekend does not materialise.** The pacing bots
   (`PacingBots.weekendPlan`, `ProgressionBots`) plan a friends weekend
   every month while the founder is single. Letting that write three
   people into state — and raise the flag that opens four new life events
   — moved `theDominatorMeetsTheIncumbent` and
   `theFounderAloneOnTheJobIsTheDominantWayToTakeIt`, i.e. the shipped
   balance. So `spendWeekend` no-ops until the roster exists; once the
   player has picked up the phone once, the weekend does exactly what the
   doc asks (the least-seen friend gets `weekendBond`, the other two get
   `weekendBystanderBond`). Verified: with this in place, every suite is
   green with no re-pins.

Net effect: **a run that never touches a friend is byte-for-byte the run
that shipped.** No test was re-pinned in this lane.

Smaller deviations:

- The hire path builds a `Candidate` and calls `EmployeeSystem.hire`, as
  the doc asks, then writes the bond onto the resulting `Employee`
  (`founderBond`) — `Candidate` has no bond field, and `founderBond` is
  what `NetworkingSystem.joinTeam` sets. If the headcount cap refuses the
  hire the candidate is removed again, so the hiring desk never quietly
  gains a friend.
- No `BalanceConfig` entry. Every number lives in `FriendTuning.standard`
  in `Friends.swift`; nothing here is read by a run that does not engage,
  so there is no default for the bots to drift on and no new balance key
  to keep at identity.
- The `friend_*` beats move bond through the hook rather than through a
  new `EventEffect` case: `EventEffect` lives in `TycoonContent` and is not
  mine.

## The numbers

| Thing | Value |
|---|---|
| Starting bond | uni friend 60, ex-colleague 52, neighbour 45 |
| Decay | 0.12/day after 7 days of silence; frozen once they move abroad or join the payroll |
| "Stops texting" | bond falls below 25 → one last line, then quiet |
| Call | free, +4 bond, once per 7 days |
| An evening | 1 evening, −$60, +9 bond, relationships +6, mood +4, energy −5; also clears a loneliness run |
| Friends weekend | +6 bond to the least-seen, +1.5 to the other two |
| Beat bumps | wedding +5, their company +3, their burnout +2, an old friend's visit +6 |
| **Hire gate** | bond ≥ 70, not the neighbour, a desk free; arrives with the whole bond as `founderBond`; +4 bond |
| **Invest gate** | bond ≥ 60, they must have started something, min cheque $2,000, one stake each; stake = amount/valuation capped at 25%; +6 bond |
| **Borrow gate** | bond ≥ 75; ceiling `bond × $200` minus what is outstanding (≈$15,000 at bond 75); no interest |
| Loan term | 182 days, then −0.25 bond/day on top of the silence decay; clearing it is +5 |
| Buyout opinion | bond ≥ 70, once per pending offer, text only |
| Their company | valuation `$60,000 + (seed >> 16) % $340,000`, name derived from the seed |

## Follow-ups in files I do not own

1. **`LifeScreen.consumeRoute` has no marker.** The scaffold marked the
   cards, the destination enum and the destination switch, but not the
   function that consumes a route — a `Route` nothing consumes does not
   work. I added a small `// MARK: Iteration 9 — L4 (friends)` region
   inside it (two branches: the launch route and `router.take`). Every
   other lane with a pushed screen needs the same; the PM may want to make
   that a proper scaffolded marker before merging.
2. **L1 (phone).** Everything this lane says arrives through
   `PhoneState.post(… from: .friend(id) …)` and is already in the thread,
   but nothing reads a friend's *name* for the thread header — L1 will
   want `state.friend(id, content:)` (public, in `Friends.swift`) for the
   title and `appearanceSeed` for the portrait. Note the roster may be
   derived rather than stored, so read it through `friendRoster(content:)`,
   never `state.life.friends.friends`.
3. **L2 (life score).** The friends component should read
   `state.friendRoster(content:)`'s bond sum (or
   `state.life.friends.bondTotal` for the *stored* roster only) — but if
   the score reads the derived roster it will be non-zero for every run,
   including the pacing bots. Since the score is display-only that is
   probably fine; if it is not, read `state.life.friends.bondTotal`, which
   is zero until the player engages.
4. **L3 (children).** A friend's wedding attended is on L3's memory list.
   The hook fires on `friends_wedding` / `friend_wedding_invite`; L3 can
   watch the same cooldown stamp or read `state.life.friends`.
5. **L1/L3 merge.** `LifeEvents.json` and `Names.json` are both append-only
   for me; my entries sit at the very end.
