## What I built

### C1 — Life in five folded sections

`Screens/Life/LifeSection.swift` (new) holds the container, the rows
and the summaries. `LifeScreen.swift` now builds Life from five sections
in the week's order.

| Section | Open on a fresh install | What it holds | Folded, it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **This week** | yes | The Fortnight and Your week as **one card**, then the doors and Inside (both draw nothing while empty), today's activities, and one **"The weekend"** row that unfolds to the weekend plan and last weekend | "3 evenings left · Round 50 can ship today · output ×0.95" |
| **People** | yes | Phone, Partner, Family, Friends, Networking: one `.row` card each, one number each, each pushing its page | "Priya Raman 52 ♥ · 2 kids · 1 asking", badged by the phone threads that are asking |
| **Money and home** | no | Personal money, Home, Things you own | "Wallet $16,420 · rent $700 in 7d" |
| **You** | no | Attributes, Wellbeing | "Output ×0.96 · energy 100" |
| **More of your life** | yes | The seven rooms. Open rooms are rows that unfold to their card, and a room that waits on you is lit and listed first. The rest sit under a quiet **"Other rooms"** row | "1 room open · 6 not opened yet", badged by the rooms that wait |

- **Remembered per install.** Every fold is a `GameSettings` key under
  `settings.lifeFold.*`: the five sections, each room's row, "Other
  rooms" and "The weekend". Nothing the player folds pops back open.
- **Both types are kept and nested.** `AgendaCard` gains `nested`, which
  draws its rows without the card's paper and header. `ThisWeekCard`
  gains `fortnight: AgendaCard?` and draws it at the top. Both defaults
  draw exactly what they drew before.
- **People's pages.**
  - Phone and Friends push the pages they always had.
  - Partner, Family and Networking had a card and no page, so their rows
    push `LifeDestination.lifeCard(_)`: the same card, alone on a page
    (`LifeCardPageView`). The partner's ten actions, the children, the
    baby button, the address book and the networking floor are all where
    they were, one tap in.
  - The Phone row appears once somebody has written, as the card did.
- **Nothing that owns a hook leaves the hierarchy.**
  - A closed section still draws its hook-owning cards invisibly
    (`lifeHook()`: zero height, hidden, no hit testing).
  - A folded room row does the same with its card.
  - `ChildrenLink` (L3's `.children` route) moves into the root's
    `.background`.
  - `HomeCard` opens its city, furnish and loftpack sheets from launch
    arguments, so a launch that asks for one of those opens Money and
    home for that launch (`forcedOpen`, not remembered).
- **Weights.** Rows use `CardWeight.row` and the quiet rows
  `CardWeight.quiet`. Until V3 lands they render as `.primary`, which is
  why a row in these screenshots is a two-line card of about 90 points.
  V3 makes them 56 points.

## Deviations

1. **An open room is a row that unfolds to its card.** It does not push
   its page, and it is not always its full card. C1 says "the rooms, one
   row each", section E says "only the rooms that are open, each a row",
   and C2 says a room's card draws once it opens. A row that unfolds to
   the card is the one reading that keeps all three, and it keeps the
   card's buttons (the side project's evening, the sabbatical report)
   one tap away. The chevron points down for these rows, and right for
   the People rows that push.
2. **The weekend plan and last weekend fold behind one row inside This
   week.** Neither C1 nor C2 names them. Section E's This week lists
   "schedule, weekend, founder output, and today's four activities", and
   Your week already says what is planned. The eight-option grid was about
   1,000 points of every first scroll.
3. **The doors and Inside sit in This week**, not More of your life. An
   open door has a 14-day clock, which makes it the week's business, and
   the audit asked to "stop hiding the card at position 25".
4. **The target is not met exactly.** See above: the activity grid, and
   rows drawn at `.primary` height until V3.
5. **"Your life" opens at the walk-away day.** The life score has no
   state to be "non-empty", so it becomes a room on the day walking away
   becomes possible to plan for (`walkAwayMinDay`, 546), or earlier if
   walking away is actually open.
6. **The pace line opens the Products tab, not a control.** That was the
   brief until V2's header control exists.
7. **C1 was not built as its own tree.** The build of C1's state ran
   with C2's card guards still in the working tree: a shell slip, since
   zsh does not split `$CARDS`. C1 alone differs only in seven card
   bodies being the scaffold's, which reference nothing new. The branch
   tip is what the suites ran on.

## Follow-ups

1. **The activity grid is the last big block on Life.** 16 numbers, on
   both folds, on every save. A compact Today card (name and price per
   tile, effects on tap) would bring the second fold to about 20. It is
   `ActivitiesCard`'s body, which is not V1's to change this round.
2. **V2, at merge:** point `ThisWeekCard.paceLine` at the Products
   header control. It is one line today, `injectedRouter?.tab =
   .products`, inside V1 markers.
3. **V3, at merge:**
   - `LifeRow` puts its one line in `CardView`'s content: the number,
     then a spacer, then the chevron. If `.row` lays title and content
     side by side, it works as is.
   - The closed section header is its own block (a card-coloured
     rounded rectangle), not a `CardView`. It may want the `.quiet`
     look.
4. **Two more tips still give directions.** `tip.state.married` says
   "…on the Life tab" and `tip.state.complaint` says "…on the Team tab".
   They were outside the four; the same neutral rewrite applies.
5. **`make strings`.** New keys:
   - the five section titles and their summary lines;
   - the row values ("N asking", "N unread", "N friends", "N contacts",
     "At the …", "♥");
   - "Other rooms", "N not opened yet", the seven dormant lines and the
     seven open lines;
   - "The weekend", "… planned";
   - "The team's pace", "on Products";
   - the four tip strings;
   - "Networking" and "Family" as page titles.
6. **People's three card pages** (partner, family, networking) are the
   old cards on a page. They work, but a real page per person, like the
   phone's, would read better.
7. **The `-autoLifeOffset` shots draw content over the rail.** The
   offset moves content, not the scroll position, so in tiles after the
   first the rail and the content overlap. This happens only with the
   debug flag.
